Logistics and supply chain services in Romania cover the commercial function of planning, moving, storing, clearing and coordinating goods across road, rail, inland waterway, sea, air and multimodal networks. The function includes freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics, third-party logistics (3PL), distribution, customs handling and carrier coordination, together with the licensing and compliance layers that determine whether an operator may lawfully perform regulated transport activity.

Romania's location on the EU's eastern frontier, Black Sea coast, Danube connection, land borders with EU and non-EU states, industrial and automotive supply chains, and road links to Central and Southeastern Europe make cross-border transport, customs, port, inland-waterway and multimodal logistics central to the professional function. The Romanian Road Authority (Autoritatea Rutieră Română, ARR) issues domestic and international transport operator licences, Community Licences and certified copies for vehicles. Commercial freight operations are subject to the EU professional-access conditions of stable establishment, good repute, financial standing and professional competence, usually through a designated transport manager holding the relevant Certificate of Professional Competence.

Romania is part of the EU Customs Union. For trade with countries outside the EU, the Romanian Customs Authority (Autoritatea Vamală Română) administers EORI identity, import, export, transit, tariff classification and customs procedures under the Union Customs Code. Romanian EORI applications are completed in the EORI-RO system, registered, printed, signed and sent to the competent regional customs directorate for the applicant's territorial area. The EORI number is unique throughout the EU and is used in customs operations in all member states.

For international businesses, the Romanian logistics environment should be assessed early alongside supply chain design, Black Sea port or warehouse selection, freight procurement, carrier and 3PL contracting, customs strategy, vehicle deployment, border and Danube routing, and EU single-market obligations. An ARR licence, Community Licence, freight-forwarding licence or EORI registration does not replace other approvals, and a change in fleet, vehicle category, goods type, trade lane, customs procedure or operating model can require a fresh regulatory and operational assessment.

Object Definition

Logistics and supply chain services in Romania form the commercial function concerned with the planning, physical movement, storage, customs clearance and coordination of goods for hire or reward, and with determining which licences, registrations and compliance measures apply to a given transport mode, service type, trade lane or goods category. The function is broader than moving a shipment: it connects freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics, 3PL coordination, Black Sea and Danube intermodal planning, carrier licensing, customs classification, transport documentation, liability rules and ongoing regulatory supervision.

DefinitionThe commercial and regulatory function covering logistics coordination, freight transport, freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics, 3PL services and customs clearance for goods moving into, out of or within Romania.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of the logistics and supply chain function is to ensure that the movement, storage, forwarding and customs clearance of goods are conducted reliably, lawfully and to commercial expectations, by operators who meet applicable establishment, repute, financial and professional standards where regulation applies. The function establishes how goods flow through the supply chain, which party is responsible for each stage, and what documentation, reporting and liability obligations apply.

PurposeTo ensure reliable, lawful and properly documented planning, movement, storage, forwarding and customs clearance of goods within, into and out of Romania.
Primary OutcomeA functioning logistics or supply chain arrangement — supported, where relevant, by an ARR transport licence, Community Licence and vehicle copies, freight-forwarding licence, transport-manager appointment, EORI-RO identity, customs authorisation or warehousing agreement — that defines the operational and legal position of the carrier, forwarder, warehouse operator, contract-logistics or 3PL provider, or trader.
Business ValueWell-structured logistics arrangements and early regulatory clarity can reduce shipment delay, border disruption, customs penalties, contractual liability exposure and later enforcement or audit risk.

Request Contexts, Users and Scenarios

Logistics and supply chain work is normally activated by a new trade lane, fleet expansion, port or warehouse requirement, or entry into cross-border freight, forwarding or customs activity. The initial question is not simply whether an ARR licence is required, but how the goods flow should be organised, whether the carrier or forwarder falls within a regulated licence category, whether a Black Sea, Danube or eastern-border route is involved, and whether it changes the legal scope of an existing authorisation or commercial arrangement.

Typical UserRoad-haulage operators, freight forwarders, customs brokers, warehouse, fulfilment and 3PL operators, contract-logistics providers, Black Sea and Danube logistics participants, manufacturers with in-house logistics, e-commerce importers, investors and foreign companies establishing Romanian freight or distribution operations.
Business EventNew haulage fleet, EU, Balkan, Black Sea, Danube or eastern-border trade lane, warehouse or fulfilment-centre establishment, 3PL contracting, ARR transport or freight-forwarding licence application, transport-manager appointment, acquisition of a licensed carrier, new import/export product line, customs-warehouse expansion or cabotage operation.
Typical ScenarioA company plans to carry goods for third parties in Romania and must obtain an ARR transport operator licence and, for international operations, a Community Licence with certified vehicle copies; a business intends to intermediate paid road transport and applies for an ARR freight-forwarding licence; an importer completes the EORI-RO application before third-country trade; a manufacturer appoints a 3PL provider for Romanian distribution and must allocate warehouse, customs and delivery responsibilities.

Country Characteristics

Romania's logistics environment is shaped by its EU external-border position, Black Sea coast, Danube connection, land routes into Central and Southeastern Europe, manufacturing and automotive supply chains, and EU Customs Union membership. ARR is the principal road-transport licensing authority and issues domestic and international operator licences, Community Licences, certified copies and freight-forwarding licences. The EORI route is administered by the Romanian Customs Authority through EORI-RO and territorially competent regional customs directorates. These separate, formal authority routes are material to the way logistics operators are established and administered in Romania.

Operational CultureDocumentation-led, territorially administered and cross-border intensive. Carrier, transport-manager, vehicle, EORI-RO, customs, warehouse, port and client data must remain consistent across Romanian, EU and counterpart-jurisdiction compliance layers.
Institutional StructureARR administers road transport, Community Licence, vehicle-copy and freight-forwarding licences through territorial agencies. The Romanian Customs Authority administers EORI and customs procedures through regional customs directorates. Port, Danube, border and infrastructure authorities are relevant to particular operations.
Classification LogicCommercial road haulage requires the appropriate ARR operator licence, while international eligible carriage requires a Community Licence and certified copies for individual vehicles. A separate ARR freight-forwarding licence is relevant to activities involving intermediation of paid road transport operations. Customs treatment depends on tariff classification, origin, value and EU/non-EU trade status.
Language ExpectationRomanian is central to ARR licensing, EORI-RO applications, regional customs and domestic authority correspondence. English is commonly used in international freight, port and forwarding operations, but formal filings and contracts should use precise Romanian terminology or professionally controlled translations.

Applicable Legislation

EU Regulations 1071/2009 and 1072/2009 form the principal framework for access to the road-haulage profession and the international road-haulage market. The Union Customs Code governs customs treatment throughout the EU, including Romania. Romania's road transport legislation and ARR regulations provide the domestic route for transport operator licensing, Community Licence administration and freight-forwarding licensing. Freight forwarding is therefore both a commercial coordination service and, when it involves intermediation of paid road transport under the relevant Romanian regime, a licensable professional activity. Warehousing and 3PL arrangements are generally organised through commercial contract unless a connected activity is separately regulated.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

There is no single universal logistics process because the appropriate route depends on service type, transport mode, vehicle category, company seat, freight-forwarding role and trade lane. Nevertheless, most Romanian operations move from planning and classification into company and ARR licence preparation, assembly of operational and financial material, formal transport, forwarding, customs or contracting setup, review, decision and ongoing compliance with licence conditions, customs obligations or service agreements.

1. Define the OperationIdentify the service type (carrier, forwarding, warehousing, fulfilment, contract logistics, 3PL), transport mode, vehicle category, goods type, Romanian establishment, Black Sea, Danube or border interface, trade lane and whether movement is domestic, intra-EU or third-country.
2. Screen Legal TriggersAssess the activity against ARR carrier or freight-forwarding licence rules, EU Community Licence and cabotage rules, vehicle-copy requirements, EORI-RO and Union Customs Code requirements, transit, sanctions and other relevant regimes.
3. Identify the Competent Authority or CounterpartyDetermine whether the matter falls to the territorial ARR agency, Romanian Customs Authority, regional customs directorate, port or Danube authority, a warehouse or 3PL provider, or another party.
4. Register and Prepare EvidenceConfirm Romanian company seat and operating premises; appoint a qualified transport manager where required; assemble good-repute, professional competence, financial standing, vehicle and corporate evidence; complete the EORI-RO registration process where relevant.
5. Prepare the Documentation BaseDevelop transport operator, Community Licence or freight-forwarding licence applications, certified-vehicle-copy material, transport and warehousing contracts, tariff-classification records, customs declarations and proposed operating conditions.
6. Submit, Contract and CompleteFile the ARR application with the territorial agency competent for the enterprise headquarters; complete, print, sign and submit the EORI-RO application to the competent regional customs directorate; finalise forwarding or warehousing agreements; address requests for clarification or supplementary material.
7. ExaminationThe authority or counterparty reviews the material, may request further evidence, and assesses establishment, good repute, financial standing, professional competence, customs classification or service terms.
8. Decision and ConditionsAn ARR transport licence, Community Licence, certified vehicle copy, freight-forwarding licence, EORI confirmation, customs authorisation or service agreement may set scope, validity, reporting duties, cabotage limits and other operating conditions.
9. Operate, Monitor and Manage ChangeMaintain compliance, keep vehicle copies available where required, meet customs and reporting duties, and assess whether fleet, service scope, trade lane or goods-category changes require renewal, amendment or a new process.
Decision logic: First identify whether the undertaking acts as carrier, freight forwarder, warehouse/3PL provider or trader, then identify the transport mode, vehicle category and trade lane. Determine the applicable ARR, Community Licence, freight-forwarding, customs and contractual regime before the operational route, authority and documentation package are finalised.

Timeline

Logistics setup, ARR licensing and customs preparation should be treated as part of supply chain planning rather than a late administrative step. Timing depends on the completeness of company, operating-premises, transport-manager, professional-capacity, financial and vehicle material, the practice of the territorial ARR agency, EORI-RO readiness, regional customs processing, border complexity and the goods category involved. EORI should be obtained before relevant customs activity begins.

Change StageBefore fleet expansion, new trade lanes, service-scope changes, new goods categories, altered forwarding activity or a changed Romanian establishment, reassess existing licences, customs procedures, registrations and agreements.

Cross-Border Relevance

Logistics and supply chain operations in Romania are inherently cross-border. Romania participates in the EU single market and Customs Union, while its Black Sea coast, Danube connection and external EU borders create heightened relevance for customs, transit, border capacity, sanctions, security and route resilience. Romanian carriers operate across EU corridors using Community Licences, and the country provides a gateway between Central Europe, the Balkans, Black Sea routes and eastern markets. Foreign investors, group companies, carriers, forwarders, warehouse operators, customs representatives and 3PL providers need clarity on which entity holds the ARR licence, Community Licence, vehicle copies, freight-forwarding licence, EORI identity and customs responsibilities.

Foreign CompaniesA foreign-owned undertaking established in Romania is generally assessed under the same Romanian and EU framework as a domestic operator. A genuine Romanian establishment, operating premises, qualified transport manager, good repute and financial standing are material to ARR licensing. Foreign entities without an EU establishment follow a separate territorial route for EORI application where Romania is the place of planned customs activity.
EU ContextA Romanian Community Licence supports eligible international road haulage throughout the EU/EEA, subject to cabotage, driver, vehicle and other applicable rules. It is distinct from domestic ARR operator licensing and from the separate freight-forwarding licence applicable to regulated paid-transport intermediation.
Black Sea, Danube and Eastern Border ContextBlack Sea ports, the Danube corridor and borders with non-EU jurisdictions make customs, transit, port, border capacity, sanctions, security and multimodal planning material to many supply chains. The exact position depends on the goods, route, border point, counterparties and applicable EU measures.
Customs ContextRomania is part of the EU Customs Union. A Romanian EORI assigned by the Romanian Customs Authority is valid throughout the EU and is used for customs operations across the Union. Goods entering or leaving the EU customs territory require the appropriate customs procedure and supporting documentation.
Language ConsiderationsRomanian is central to ARR licensing, EORI-RO and regional customs interaction. English is common in international freight, Black Sea port activity and commercial logistics, but formal submissions, licences, customs records and contracts should use precise Romanian terminology or controlled translations.
Practical RiskAssuming that a Community Licence replaces domestic ARR carrier or freight-forwarding licensing, that EORI identity eliminates customs, transit or border obligations, or that a foreign authorisation automatically satisfies Romanian establishment and professional-access requirements without separate assessment.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central practical risk is treating Romanian logistics licensing and customs compliance as a one-time filing exercise rather than an ongoing operational and compliance process. Errors in carrier-versus-forwarder classification, ARR licence scope, transport-manager arrangements, EORI-RO documentation, customs or service records and insufficient attention to border, port, Danube, carrier and contractual conditions can affect timing, cost and the ability to operate as planned.

Carrier and Forwarder Classification RiskAn undertaking may act as a regulated road freight forwarder without the appropriate ARR forwarding licence, or treat a paid carriage model as an unregulated coordination service when an operator licence is required.
Community Licence and Vehicle Copy RiskInternational commercial road freight requires a valid Community Licence and certified copy for each vehicle. Fleet additions, substitutions or document-control failures can create compliance exposure.
Transport Manager and Establishment RiskThe transport manager must hold the required professional competence and be properly appointed. The undertaking must maintain its Romanian establishment, operating premises, financial standing and good-repute position.
EORI-RO and Customs RiskEORI-RO applications require a formal electronic and documentary sequence. Incorrect tariff classification, customs value, origin treatment, EORI data or procedure selection can affect duty, VAT, release timing and customs audit exposure.
Eastern Border and Transit RiskThird-country trade lanes and eastern-border operations can be affected by customs, transit, sanctions, security, congestion and changing operational restrictions. These should be incorporated into route planning, service contracts and contingency arrangements.
Cost DriversARR licence, forwarding licence and vehicle-copy fees, professional competence and financial-standing evidence, customs duties and import VAT, port and terminal charges, warehouse and 3PL fees, tolls, IT and declaration systems, border contingencies, professional advisers and potential penalties.

FAQ

Which authority issues road transport licences in Romania?ARR, the Romanian Road Authority, issues domestic and international road transport operator licences, Community Licences and certified copies. Applications are generally filed with the territorial ARR agency for the area in which the enterprise has its headquarters.
Does an international Romanian road carrier need a Community Licence?Yes. Eligible international commercial road freight is carried out under the Community Licence framework. ARR issues the Community Licence and the certified copies needed for the individual vehicles used in the international transport operation.
Is freight forwarding separately licensed in Romania?Yes, where the activity involves the intermediation of paid road transport operations under the Romanian regulatory framework. ARR issues a freight-forwarding licence on application. The application commonly requires company documents, premises evidence, transport-manager appointment, professional competence, criminal-record documentation, financial capacity and proof of fee payment.
How does EORI registration work in Romania?The applicant completes the EORI application in the EORI-RO system on the Romanian Customs Authority website. The application is registered, printed, signed and sent to the regional customs directorate competent for the applicant's territorial area. The assigned EORI is valid throughout the EU.
Are freight forwarding and warehousing regulated in the same way?No. Romanian road freight forwarding can be a separate licensed intermediation activity under ARR rules. Warehousing, contract logistics and 3PL services are generally organised through commercial contract, although connected activities such as customs warehousing, customs representation, dangerous-goods handling or environmental operation may carry their own separate requirements.
Why do Black Sea and Danube routes require separate attention?Black Sea, Danube and eastern-border routes can involve different port, inland-waterway, transit, customs, security, sanctions and congestion factors than an ordinary intra-EU road route. The operational and legal route should be assessed for the specific goods, vehicle, border point and contracting structure.
