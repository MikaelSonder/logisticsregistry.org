Logistics and supply chain services in China cover the commercial function of planning, moving, storing, clearing and coordinating goods across road, rail, inland waterway, coastal and international sea, air and multimodal networks. The function includes road freight, freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics, third-party logistics (3PL), distribution, customs handling and carrier coordination, together with the licensing, filing, registration and compliance layers that determine whether an operator may lawfully perform regulated logistics activity.

China's scale, manufacturing depth, regional logistics markets, port network, inland waterway system, cross-border e-commerce, free trade zones and land connections to Central Asia and Southeast Asia make domestic distribution, port/airport operations, customs and multimodal planning central to the professional function. Road freight transport and road freight station (yard) operations are regulated through national and local transport administration. The actual authorisation route depends on whether the undertaking carries goods, operates a freight station, acts as a freight forwarder, provides multimodal transport or carries out a specialised or dangerous-goods service.

For foreign trade, the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (GACC) administers the registration and filing of import/export goods consignors and consignees, customs declaration enterprises, customs declarations, tariff classification, bonded arrangements and customs controls. Enterprise qualification filing is managed through the China International Trade Single Window or the Internet + Customs platform. The Single Window enables a single electronic submission for cargo and tax declaration and supports the electronic status and filing record of consignors, consignees and customs declaration enterprises. China does not use the EU EORI system.

For international businesses, the Chinese logistics environment should be assessed early alongside supply chain design, Chinese entity and business-scope planning, port, airport, warehouse or bonded-zone selection, freight procurement, carrier and 3PL contracting, customs filing, importer/exporter arrangements and Chinese-language administration. A road freight licence, customs filing, bonded warehouse approval or Single Window account does not replace other approvals, and a change in business scope, goods category, facility, trade lane, customs procedure or operating model can require a fresh regulatory and operational assessment.

Object Definition

Logistics and supply chain services in China form the commercial function concerned with the planning, physical movement, storage, customs clearance and coordination of goods across domestic and international networks, and with determining which business scopes, permits, filings, registrations and compliance measures apply to a given service role, transport mode, goods category, facility or trade lane. The function is broader than moving a shipment: it connects road freight, freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics, 3PL coordination, bonded status, customs filing, Single Window declarations, transport documentation, liability rules and ongoing regulatory supervision.

DefinitionThe commercial and regulatory function covering logistics coordination, road freight, freight forwarding, warehousing, bonded storage, contract logistics, 3PL services and customs clearance for goods moving into, out of or within China.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of the logistics and supply chain function is to ensure that the movement, storage, forwarding, bonded handling and customs clearance of goods are conducted reliably, lawfully and to commercial expectations, by entities with the appropriate business scope, permits, filings, Customs status and operational capacity. The function establishes how goods flow through the supply chain, which party is responsible for each stage, and what Chinese customs, trade, facility, reporting and liability obligations apply.

PurposeTo ensure reliable, lawful and properly documented planning, movement, storage, forwarding, bonded handling and customs clearance of goods within, into, out of or through China.
Primary OutcomeA functioning logistics or supply chain arrangement — supported, where relevant, by Chinese company and business-scope registration, road freight permission or filing, customs enterprise filing, China International Trade Single Window access, bonded warehouse or zone approval, customs procedure or service agreement — that defines the operational and legal position of the carrier, forwarder, warehouse operator, bonded operator, 3PL provider, customs declaration enterprise, consignee, consignor or trader.
Business ValueWell-structured logistics arrangements and early regulatory clarity can reduce cargo delay, customs and tax errors, product-control failures, contractual liability exposure, working-capital disruption and later enforcement or audit risk.

The initial question is not simply whether a permit is required, but whether the undertaking acts as carrier, freight forwarder, warehouse or bonded warehouse operator, customs declaration enterprise, importer/exporter consignee or consignor, 3PL provider or trader, and whether the proposed activity is within the entity's approved business scope and existing customs filing.

Country Characteristics

China's logistics environment is shaped by the scale and regional diversity of the market, extensive manufacturing and export supply chains, major seaports and airports, inland waterways, land-border corridors, free trade zones and an integrated but multi-agency digital customs environment. The actual service and facility classification is decisive: carrier, freight station operator, forwarder, warehouse operator, bonded warehouse operator, customs declaration enterprise, consignee/consignor and 3PL provider do not have interchangeable roles. A Chinese company must also ensure that its business scope, corporate registration, official seals, tax, customs and operational model remain aligned.

Operational CultureDocumentation-led, digitally enabled, locally administered and role-specific. Chinese-language company, customs, goods, facility, contract, seal and declaration data must remain consistent across market regulation, transport, customs, port, airport and product-control systems.
Institutional StructureLocal transport authorities administer road freight and station activity under national Ministry of Transport rules. GACC and local Customs administer enterprise filing, declarations, bonded controls and customs procedures. The China International Trade Single Window and Internet + Customs provide principal electronic channels. SAMR registers the enterprise and its business scope.
Classification LogicThe legal route depends on the actual service: road freight operation, freight station/yard operation, freight forwarding, warehousing, bonded warehousing, customs declaration enterprise, consignee/consignor or 3PL. Import/export customs capability depends on customs filing rather than a single universal import/export licence. Product-specific registration, inspection, quarantine, tax, foreign-exchange and licensing rules may also apply.
Language ExpectationChinese is central to corporate registration, business scope, transport filings, customs declarations, Single Window records, seals, contracts and authority correspondence. English-language information is available for selected government and local investment materials, but legally operative filings and operational documents should use precise Chinese terminology and controlled translations.

Applicable Legislation

China does not apply the EU Community Licence or Union Customs Code framework. Logistics and supply chain activity is governed by Chinese national legislation and local implementation involving transport, customs, company registration, foreign trade, bonded facilities and product control. The Road Transport Regulations and associated Road Freight Transport and Station Administration Provisions create the main road freight framework; the Customs Law governs import/export and customs control; and Customs filing rules govern the registration of consignors, consignees and customs declaration enterprises. The actual business role and place of operation determine the applicable legal set.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

There is no single universal logistics process because the correct route depends on the service role, province or municipality, transport mode, goods category, facility, business scope and trade lane. Nevertheless, most China operations move from entity and operating-model classification into business-scope and transport preparation, Customs enterprise filing and Single Window setup, facility and contract development, customs or transport approvals, operational testing and ongoing compliance.

1. Define the OperationIdentify whether the undertaking acts as road carrier, freight station operator, forwarder, warehouse or bonded warehouse operator, customs declaration enterprise, import/export consignee or consignor, 3PL provider or trader; define goods, facility, customs status, port/airport interface and trade lane.
2. Confirm Chinese Entity and Business ScopeConfirm the Chinese legal entity, business licence, approved business scope, registered address, investors, legal representative, company seals and actual operational capacity needed for the proposed logistics or trade activity.
3. Screen Legal TriggersAssess the activity against road freight and freight station rules, local transport procedures, customs enterprise filing, bonded/FTZ requirements, product-specific controls, import/export, tax, foreign-exchange and other relevant regimes.
4. Identify the Competent Authority or CounterpartyDetermine whether the matter falls to a local transport authority, GACC/local Customs, the Single Window, SAMR, a port/airport/bonded-zone authority, a customs declaration enterprise, warehouse or 3PL provider, or another agency.
5. Prepare the Documentation BaseDevelop business licence and business-scope materials, transport filings or permits, customs enterprise information, Single Window account and qualification data, tariff/classification records, declarations, facility documents and commercial contracts.
6. File, Register, Contract and CompleteComplete local road freight or station formalities; file consignee/consignor or customs declaration enterprise information through the China International Trade Single Window or Internet + Customs; arrange bonded/facility procedures; finalise carrier, forwarding, warehouse and 3PL agreements.
7. Examination and VerificationThe authority or counterparty reviews the material, may request further evidence, and assesses business scope, location, facilities, goods, customs status, declaration role, transport conditions or service terms.
8. Customs Clearance and Operational LaunchSubmit cargo and tax declaration data through the Single Window or prescribed channel; complete inspection, quarantine or product-control requirements; release, store, distribute, export or transfer goods under the selected procedure and contract model.
9. Operate, Monitor and Manage ChangeMaintain company, Customs filing, transport, facility, Single Window, customs declaration, warehouse, bonded, product-control and recordkeeping compliance; reassess before changing business scope, goods, facilities, trade lanes, customs status or service roles.
Decision logic: First identify the Chinese entity, actual service role and goods/customs status. Then confirm whether the business scope and local transport route support the activity and whether the enterprise must be filed as an import/export consignor/consignee or customs declaration enterprise. Only after that classification can the appropriate authority, China International Trade Single Window procedures, document package and contractual responsibilities be planned reliably.

Timeline

Chinese logistics entity setup, business-scope alignment, road freight formalities, Customs filing and facility approvals should be treated as part of supply chain design rather than a late administrative step. Timing depends on the city and province, enterprise structure, business scope, location, goods type, Customs district, bonded or free-trade-zone model, product-control requirements and the completeness of Chinese-language documentation. Customs enterprise filing may be integrated with business registration in eligible cases, but specialised transport, bonded, warehouse and product approvals need their own analysis.

Cross-Border Relevance

Logistics and supply chain operations in China are inherently cross-border because China is a major global manufacturing, import/export, port, air-cargo and e-commerce jurisdiction. China is outside the EU, EEA and EU Customs Union; Chinese transport permissions, Customs filing, Single Window procedures and importer/exporter identification are distinct from EU Community Licence and EORI systems. Foreign investors, group companies, carriers, forwarders, warehouse operators, Customs declaration enterprises and 3PL providers need clarity on which Chinese entity acts as consignee, consignor, customs declarant, importer/exporter, contract party, goods controller and Customs risk bearer.

Foreign CompaniesA foreign company intending to directly conduct logistics or import/export activity in China normally needs an appropriate Chinese entity, branch or authorised commercial structure with a business scope and Customs filing aligned to its actual role. A foreign company should not assume that an overseas freight forwarder, EORI, global 3PL agreement or foreign importer-of-record model automatically creates Chinese customs or business authority.
China-EU and International ContextChina does not use the EU Community Licence or EORI system. Chinese road freight, customs filing and Single Window procedures are national. EU-side transport, EORI, VAT, Customs and product requirements remain separate for goods moving into or through EU jurisdictions.
Importer, Exporter and Customs Declarant ContextThe consignee/consignor of imported/exported goods and the customs declaration enterprise may be the same or different entities. The business scope, Customs filing, Customs declaration authority, commercial contract and goods ownership/risk allocation should be aligned before Customs declarations are filed.
Port, Airport, Bonded and Free Trade Zone ContextChinese ports, airports, bonded warehouses and free trade zones can provide different Customs, inventory, tax, storage and re-export models. The exact advantage and compliance burden depend on the facility, goods, Customs district, procedure and counterparties.
Language ConsiderationsChinese is central to company registration, Customs enterprise filing, Single Window submissions, Customs declarations, product controls, facility approvals and contracts. English-language information may explain the process, but legally operative filings and key operating documents should be controlled in Chinese.
Practical RiskAssuming that a Chinese business licence alone creates import/export Customs capability, that a foreign Customs identifier can be used in China, or that a 3PL/forwarding agreement automatically defines the Chinese consignee, customs declarant, product-control, tax and Customs liability roles without separate assessment.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central practical risk is treating Chinese logistics, transport, corporate registration, Customs filing and commercial contracts as separate administrative tasks rather than a single integrated operating model. Errors in business scope, role classification, entity authority, Customs enterprise filing, Single Window data, product controls, bonded status or contract allocation can affect cargo release, cost, working capital and the ability to operate as planned.

Business Scope and Entity RiskThe Chinese entity's registered business scope may not support the actual intended road freight, forwarding, warehousing, import/export, Customs declaration or logistics activity. Changes in operations can require company-registration and business-scope adaptation before the service starts.
Role Classification RiskAn undertaking may misclassify itself as a general 3PL or agent when it is actually acting as road carrier, freight station operator, forwarder, Customs declaration enterprise, bonded operator, consignee or consignor, each of which can have different authority and compliance requirements.
Customs Filing and Single Window RiskIncorrect enterprise qualification data, inconsistent company, investor, management or customs declaration information, unauthorised users or incomplete filing can interrupt Customs declarations or prevent the intended entity from acting in its stated Customs role.
Customs and Product Classification RiskIncorrect tariff classification, Customs value, origin treatment, goods description, product-control documentation, inspection/quarantine data or procedure selection can affect duty, import VAT, release timing, product compliance and audit exposure.
Bonded and Warehouse RiskOrdinary warehousing, bonded warehouse, free trade zone and Customs-controlled storage have different Customs, inventory, tax, security, reporting and facility obligations. Unclear allocation of goods control and Customs status can create operational and contractual exposure.
Cross-Border and Local Implementation RiskLocal transport authority practice, Customs district procedures, port/airport processes, provincial requirements, product controls, trade measures, export controls and changing cross-border conditions can materially affect a supply chain. These variables should be built into route planning, systems and contracts.
Cost DriversEntity formation and business-scope change, transport and facility approvals, Customs duties and import taxes, port/airport/terminal charges, bonded warehouse/FTZ costs, warehouse and 3PL fees, Single Window and IT systems, product-control support, Chinese-language documentation, professional advisers and potential penalties.

FAQ

Does China use an EORI number?No. EORI is an EU customs identification system. China uses its own Customs enterprise filing and identification framework. Import/export consignors, consignees and customs declaration enterprises register or file through the China International Trade Single Window or Internet + Customs system, using the relevant Chinese enterprise and Customs data.
What is the China International Trade Single Window?The China International Trade Single Window is the national electronic platform for cross-border trade. It enables enterprises to submit cargo and tax declaration data through a single system and provides functions for enterprise qualification, Customs filing, status inquiry and related trade administration.
Does a Chinese company need a separate import/export licence?There is no single universal import/export licence that replaces all Customs and trade formalities. An enterprise intending to act as consignee or consignor of import/export goods must complete the relevant Customs registration and filing process, ensure its business scope supports the activity, and obtain any product-specific approvals that apply.
What is the difference between a consignee/consignor and a customs declaration enterprise?A consignee or consignor is the business importing or exporting goods in its own Customs role. A customs declaration enterprise provides Customs declaration services, often for other parties. An enterprise may have one or both status types where the applicable Customs rules and filing conditions are met. The customs role must align with the commercial contract and goods responsibility.
Are road freight and freight stations regulated in China?Yes. Road freight transport and road freight station/yard operations are governed by the Road Transport Regulations and the Provisions on the Administration of Road Freight Transport and Stations. The applicable licence, filing, safety and local authority route depends on the specific activity, vehicle, facility, goods and province or municipality.
What is a bonded warehouse in China?A bonded warehouse is a Customs-controlled facility in which foreign goods may be stored or handled under the applicable Customs procedure before import clearance, re-export or another permitted Customs outcome. It is distinct from ordinary commercial storage and requires its own Customs, inventory, security and reporting arrangements.
