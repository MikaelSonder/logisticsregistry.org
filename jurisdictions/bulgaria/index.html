Logistics and supply chain services in Bulgaria cover the commercial function of planning, moving, storing, clearing and coordinating goods across road, rail, inland waterway, sea, air and multimodal networks. The function includes freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics, third-party logistics (3PL), distribution, customs handling and carrier coordination, together with the licensing and compliance layers that determine whether an operator may lawfully perform regulated transport activity.

Bulgaria's position on the EU's southeastern external frontier, Black Sea coast, Danube connection, land borders with Romania, Greece, Türkiye, Serbia and North Macedonia, and role on Central European and Balkan freight corridors make cross-border road transport, customs, port, Danube and transit logistics central to the professional function. The Executive Agency Automobile Administration issues road freight transport licences for domestic Bulgarian and EU operations. The undertaking must satisfy reliability, professional competence, financial standing and vehicle fitness requirements. A licence is not required where both the vehicle payload is no more than 1.5 tonnes and maximum permissible vehicle weight is no more than 3.5 tonnes, although other transport rules can still apply.

For trade with countries outside the EU, the National Customs Agency administers EORI identity, import, export, transit, tariff classification and customs procedures under the Union Customs Code. EORI registration is available electronically through the Bulgarian Customs Agency e-Portal or through paper submission to the competent customs office. The EORI number is unique throughout the EU and is used to identify persons in customs interactions, information exchange and customs procedures. Bulgaria's Customs Agency has a central directorate and territorial customs directorates including Sofia Airport, Burgas, Varna, Lom, Plovdiv, Svilengrad, Svishtov, Sofia, Ruse and Southwestern Customs.

For international businesses, the Bulgarian logistics environment should be assessed early alongside supply chain design, Black Sea port or warehouse selection, freight procurement, carrier and 3PL contracting, customs strategy, vehicle deployment, Danube and border routing, and EU single-market obligations. A transport licence, Community Licence, EORI registration or customs arrangement does not replace other approvals, and a change in fleet, vehicle category, goods type, trade lane, customs procedure or operating model can require a fresh regulatory and operational assessment.

Object Definition

Logistics and supply chain services in Bulgaria form the commercial function concerned with the planning, physical movement, storage, customs clearance and coordination of goods for hire or reward, and with determining which licences, registrations and compliance measures apply to a given transport mode, service type, trade lane or goods category. The function is broader than moving a shipment: it connects freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics, 3PL coordination, Black Sea and Danube intermodal planning, carrier licensing, customs classification, transport documentation, liability rules and ongoing regulatory supervision.

DefinitionThe commercial and regulatory function covering logistics coordination, freight transport, freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics, 3PL services and customs clearance for goods moving into, out of or within Bulgaria.

The Registry Object covers the practical operational, licensing and compliance architecture for commercial logistics and supply chain activity in Bulgaria. It focuses on the early classification question, the competent authority where regulation applies, the information base, the operational process and the practical consequences of a Bulgarian transport licence, Community Licence, customs authorisation, warehouse arrangement or freight-forwarding engagement.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of the logistics and supply chain function is to ensure that the movement, storage, forwarding and customs clearance of goods are conducted reliably, lawfully and to commercial expectations, by operators who meet applicable establishment, reliability, financial and professional standards where regulation applies. The function establishes how goods flow through the supply chain, which party is responsible for each stage, and what documentation, reporting and liability obligations apply.

PurposeTo ensure reliable, lawful and properly documented planning, movement, storage, forwarding and customs clearance of goods within, into and out of Bulgaria.
Primary OutcomeA functioning logistics or supply chain arrangement — supported, where relevant, by a Bulgarian transport licence, Community Licence and certified vehicle copies, EORI registration, customs authorisation or warehousing agreement — that defines the operational and legal position of the carrier, forwarder, warehouse operator, contract-logistics or 3PL provider, or trader.
Business ValueWell-structured logistics arrangements and early regulatory clarity can reduce shipment delay, border disruption, customs penalties, contractual liability exposure and later enforcement or audit risk.

Request Contexts, Users and Scenarios

Logistics and supply chain work is normally activated by a new trade lane, fleet expansion, port or warehouse requirement, or entry into cross-border freight, forwarding or customs activity. The initial question is not simply whether a transport licence is required, but how the goods flow should be organised, whether the vehicle falls within the relevant Bulgarian threshold, whether an EU external-border route is involved, and whether it changes the legal scope of an existing authorisation or commercial arrangement.

Typical UserRoad-haulage operators, freight forwarders, customs brokers, warehouse, fulfilment and 3PL operators, contract-logistics providers, Black Sea and Danube logistics participants, industrial manufacturers with in-house logistics, e-commerce importers, investors and foreign companies establishing Bulgarian freight or distribution operations.
Business EventNew haulage fleet, EU, Balkan, Black Sea, Danube or Türkiye trade lane, warehouse or fulfilment-centre establishment, 3PL contracting, Bulgarian transport licence or Community Licence application, acquisition of a carrier, new import/export product line, customs-warehouse expansion or cabotage operation.
Typical ScenarioA company plans paid road freight in Bulgaria using a vehicle above the relevant licence thresholds and must obtain a transport licence from the Executive Agency Automobile Administration; an international carrier obtains a Community Licence and vehicle copies for EU freight; an importer registers for EORI through the Customs Agency e-Portal or at the competent customs office; a manufacturer appoints a 3PL provider for Bulgarian distribution and must allocate warehouse, customs and delivery responsibilities.
Professional AssistanceTypically relevant when transport licence or Community Licence status is uncertain, customs classification is complex, supply chain design spans several modes, providers and jurisdictions, or transit, sanctions, driver, cabotage and EU external-border trade rules are relevant.

Country Characteristics

Bulgaria's logistics environment is shaped by its EU external-border position, Black Sea ports, Danube connection, road and rail corridors to the Balkans and Türkiye, industrial supply chains, EU and Schengen participation and Customs Union membership. The Executive Agency Automobile Administration administers the professional road-freight licensing route, with a vehicle threshold that combines payload and maximum permissible weight. The National Customs Agency operates the customs and EORI layer through a central directorate, territorial customs houses and its electronic portal. These authority and border interfaces are central to the practical operation of logistics services in Bulgaria.

Operational CultureDocumentation-led, cross-border intensive and border-sensitive. Carrier, vehicle, customs, EORI, warehouse, port, Danube and client data must remain consistent across Bulgarian, EU and counterpart-jurisdiction compliance layers.
Institutional StructureThe Executive Agency Automobile Administration under the Ministry of Transport and Communications issues domestic and EU freight licences. The National Customs Agency administers EORI and customs through a central directorate and territorial customs houses. Port, Danube, rail and border bodies are relevant to specific operations.
Classification LogicA Bulgarian goods transport licence is required for commercial paid transport where the vehicle exceeds the relevant payload and maximum-weight thresholds. Community Licence rules apply to eligible EU international freight, with copies for individual vehicles. The licence is not required where both payload does not exceed 1.5 tonnes and maximum permissible weight does not exceed 3.5 tonnes. Customs treatment depends on tariff classification, origin, value and EU/non-EU trade status.
Language ExpectationBulgarian is central to licensing, Customs Agency and domestic authority procedures. English-language Customs Agency and e-Portal information is available for selected services, but formal applications, customs records and contracts should use precise Bulgarian terminology or controlled translations.

The competent authority depends on transport mode, company establishment and legal route. A single Bulgarian supply chain operation may interact with the Executive Agency Automobile Administration, National Customs Agency, territorial customs house, port or Danube authority, warehouse or 3PL provider and sector-specific bodies because transport licensing, customs, vehicle, warehouse and border-related matters are administered through different channels.

Applicable Legislation

EU Regulations 1071/2009 and 1072/2009 form the principal framework for access to the road-haulage profession and the international road-haulage market. The Union Customs Code governs customs treatment throughout the EU, including Bulgaria. Bulgaria's Road Transport Act and implementing regulations provide the national route for domestic and international goods transport licensing. Freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics and 3PL arrangements are generally organised through commercial contract and general Bulgarian commercial law, unless a connected activity is separately regulated.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

There is no single universal logistics process because the appropriate route depends on service type, transport mode, vehicle category, company establishment and trade lane. Nevertheless, most Bulgarian operations move from planning and classification into company and transport-licence preparation, assembly of operational and financial material, formal Community Licence, customs or contracting setup, review, decision and ongoing compliance with transport conditions, customs obligations or service agreements.

1. Define the OperationIdentify the service type (transport, forwarding, warehousing, fulfilment, contract logistics, 3PL), transport mode, vehicle payload and maximum permitted mass, goods type, Bulgarian operating base, Black Sea, Danube or border interface, trade lane and whether movement is domestic, intra-EU or third-country.
2. Screen Legal TriggersAssess the activity against Bulgarian domestic freight licence thresholds, EU Community Licence and cabotage rules, EORI and Union Customs Code requirements, transit, sanctions, port/Danube rules and other relevant regimes.
3. Identify the Competent Authority or CounterpartyDetermine whether the matter falls to the Executive Agency Automobile Administration, National Customs Agency, territorial customs directorate, port or Danube authority, a warehouse or 3PL provider, or another party.
4. Register and Prepare EvidenceConfirm Bulgarian company establishment; appoint a transport manager where required; assemble reliability, professional competence, financial-standing, vehicle-fitness and corporate evidence; obtain EORI registration through the Customs Agency e-Portal or competent customs office where relevant.
5. Prepare the Documentation BaseDevelop transport licence and Community Licence applications, certified-vehicle-copy material, transport and warehousing contracts, tariff-classification records, customs declarations and proposed operating conditions.
6. Submit, Contract and CompleteFile the transport licence or Community Licence request with the Executive Agency Automobile Administration; submit EORI registration electronically or on paper to the competent Customs authority; finalise forwarding or warehousing agreements; address requests for clarification or supplementary material.
7. ExaminationThe authority or counterparty reviews the material, may request further evidence, and assesses establishment, reliability, financial standing, professional competence, vehicle fitness, customs classification or service terms.
8. Decision and ConditionsA transport licence, Community Licence, certified vehicle copy, EORI confirmation, customs authorisation or service agreement may set scope, validity, reporting duties, cabotage limits and other operating conditions.
9. Operate, Monitor and Manage ChangeMaintain compliance, carry valid vehicle copies where required, meet customs and reporting duties, and assess whether fleet, service scope, trade lane or goods-category changes require renewal, amendment or a new process.
Decision logic: First identify the service type, transport mode, vehicle payload and maximum weight, Bulgarian operating base and trade lane. Then determine the applicable Bulgarian transport licence, EU Community Licence, customs and contractual regime. Only after the operational route is identified can the competent authority or provider, documentation package and compliance timeline be reliably planned.

Timeline

Logistics setup, road transport licensing and customs registration should be treated as part of supply chain planning rather than a late administrative step. Timing depends on the completeness of company, operating-base, professional, financial and vehicle material, Executive Agency Automobile Administration processing, Customs Agency e-Portal access, the territorial customs route, border complexity and the goods category involved. EORI should be secured before relevant customs activity begins.

Early Planning StageTrade-lane definition, transport mode and service selection, payload/vehicle-mass analysis, Bulgarian operating-base assessment, legal classification and transport licence, customs or contracting strategy.

Change StageBefore fleet expansion, new Black Sea, Danube, Balkan or third-country trade lanes, service-scope changes, new goods categories or a changed Bulgarian operating base, reassess existing licences, customs procedures, registrations and agreements.

Cross-Border Relevance

Logistics and supply chain operations in Bulgaria are inherently cross-border. Bulgaria participates in the EU single market, Customs Union and Schengen Area, while its position on the EU's external frontier, Black Sea coast and Danube corridor gives added importance to customs, transit, border management, sanctions, security and route resilience. Bulgarian carriers operate throughout EU freight corridors under Community Licences, while trade toward Türkiye, the Western Balkans and other third countries requires route-specific customs and permit analysis.

Foreign CompaniesA foreign-owned undertaking established in Bulgaria is generally assessed under the same Bulgarian and EU framework as a domestic operator. A genuine Bulgarian establishment, reliability, financial standing, professional competence and vehicle arrangements are material to the transport licence and Community Licence analysis.
EU ContextA Bulgarian Community Licence supports eligible international road haulage throughout the EU/EEA, subject to cabotage, driver, vehicle and other applicable rules. It is distinct from the national transport licence and from the different requirements applicable to third-country routes.
Black Sea, Danube and External Border ContextBlack Sea ports, Danube freight connections and Bulgarian borders with Türkiye, Serbia and North Macedonia create interfaces between EU internal movement and external customs, transit, security and route-planning requirements. The exact position depends on goods, route, border point, vehicle and applicable EU measures.
Customs ContextBulgaria is part of the EU Customs Union. A Bulgarian EORI is valid throughout the EU and is used to identify the operator in customs activity. Goods entering or leaving the EU customs territory require the applicable customs procedure and supporting documentation.
Language ConsiderationsBulgarian is central to domestic licence and Customs Agency interaction. English-language information is available from the National Customs Agency for selected matters, but formal registrations, licences, customs records and contracts should use precise Bulgarian terminology or controlled translations.
Practical RiskAssuming that a Community Licence replaces Bulgarian domestic licensing, that EORI identity eliminates border, transit or sanctions obligations, or that a foreign carrier authorisation automatically satisfies Bulgarian establishment and professional-access requirements without separate assessment.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central practical risk is treating Bulgarian logistics licensing and customs compliance as a one-time filing exercise rather than an ongoing operational and compliance process. Errors in vehicle threshold analysis, domestic/international licence scope, EORI registration, customs or service documentation and insufficient attention to border, port, Danube, carrier and contractual conditions can affect timing, cost and the ability to operate as planned.

Vehicle Threshold RiskA carrier may misapply the licence exemption by focusing on payload or maximum permissible mass alone. The exemption applies only where both payload is no more than 1.5 tonnes and maximum permissible weight is no more than 3.5 tonnes.
Professional Access RiskThe undertaking must maintain the applicable reliability, professional competence, financial standing, Bulgarian establishment and vehicle fitness requirements. Incomplete or inconsistent evidence can delay licensing or affect continuing compliance.
Community Licence and Vehicle Copy RiskInternational commercial road freight requires a valid Community Licence and certified copy for each vehicle. Fleet additions, substitutions or document-control failures can create compliance exposure.
Customs and EORI RiskIncorrect tariff classification, customs value, origin treatment, EORI data or procedure selection can affect duty, VAT, release timing and customs audit exposure. The operator should select and complete the proper e-Portal or competent customs-office route before the first relevant customs activity.
External Border and Transit RiskBlack Sea, Danube, Türkiye, Western Balkans and other third-country routes can be affected by customs, transit, sanctions, security, congestion, permits and changing operational restrictions. These should be built into route planning and service contracts.
Cost DriversLicence and certified-copy fees, professional and financial evidence, customs duties and import VAT, port and terminal charges, warehouse and 3PL fees, tolls, IT and declaration systems, border contingencies, professional advisers and potential penalties.

FAQ

When is a Bulgarian goods transport licence required?A transport licence is generally required for commercial paid goods transport when the vehicle exceeds the applicable thresholds. The exemption applies where the vehicle payload is no more than 1.5 tonnes and its maximum permissible weight is no more than 3.5 tonnes. The current vehicle and operation classification should be confirmed with the Executive Agency Automobile Administration.
What conditions must a Bulgarian road transport operator meet?The road transport operator must meet conditions of reliability, professional competence, financial standing and transport fitness of the vehicles. The operator must also have the required Bulgarian establishment and comply with the applicable domestic or EU licence route.
When is a Community Licence required in Bulgaria?A Community Licence supports eligible international commercial road freight under EU rules. It is issued to a qualified Bulgarian carrier and certified copies are issued for the vehicles used. The licence is distinct from the national Bulgarian goods transport licence and from third-country route permits where applicable.
How is EORI obtained in Bulgaria?An EORI number may be obtained electronically through the Bulgarian Customs Agency e-Portal or by submitting the prescribed registration form and supporting documents in hard copy to the appropriate customs office. The EORI is unique throughout the EU and used in customs interactions.
Are freight forwarding and warehousing separately licensed in Bulgaria?Freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics and 3PL services are generally organised through commercial contract and general Bulgarian commercial law rather than a dedicated professional licence. Connected activities such as commercial road haulage, customs warehousing, customs representation, dangerous-goods handling or port operations may carry separate requirements.
Why do Bulgaria's external borders matter to a supply chain?Bulgaria connects EU internal markets with Türkiye, the Western Balkans, Black Sea routes and other third-country markets. The same goods flow can involve EU transport law, customs, transit, sanctions, security, border capacity, port and Danube operations. The legal and operational route should be assessed for the particular goods, vehicle and counterparties.
