Logistics and supply chain services in Saudi Arabia cover the commercial function of planning, moving, storing, clearing and coordinating goods across road, rail, sea, air and multimodal networks. The function includes freight transport, freight brokerage and forwarding, customs clearance, warehousing, contract logistics, third-party logistics (3PL), distribution, transhipment and carrier coordination, together with the commercial registration, transport licence, customs, product conformity and compliance layers that determine whether an operator may lawfully perform the selected activity.

Saudi Arabia's large territory, GCC location, Red Sea and Arabian Gulf ports, land corridors with neighbouring states, industrial and energy supply chains, growing logistics infrastructure and strategic trade position make road freight, ports, airports, customs and warehousing central to the professional function. The Transport General Authority (TGA) regulates land, maritime and rail transport and issues licences for relevant transport and logistics activities through the Logisti platform. The exact licence depends on whether the undertaking is a carrier, freight broker, logistics services provider, warehouse operator or another defined transport activity. A commercial registration with the relevant logistics activity and, for certain activities, a municipality permit, GOSI certificate and ZATCA clearance certificate are part of the business-licensing route.

For cross-border trade, the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA) administers customs, import/export registration, customs broker authorisation, customs declarations, tariff classification, valuation, origin, duties and import VAT through the Fasah single-window platform. A trader registers as an importer or exporter on Fasah using its commercial registration or ID number, activates the account and may authorise a licensed customs broker for import, export or both. Importers should submit Fasah clearance data and the customs declaration at least 48 hours before cargo arrives at the port of entry, with documents including commercial invoice, bill of lading and certificate of origin, supplemented by product-specific documents where relevant.

For international businesses, the Saudi logistics environment should be assessed early alongside entity and commercial registration structure, port, airport or warehouse selection, freight procurement, carrier and 3PL contracting, TGA licensing, Fasah registration, ZATCA customs strategy, importer/exporter responsibility and product conformity. A commercial registration, TGA licence, Fasah account or customs broker authorisation does not replace other approvals. A change in activity, fleet, goods category, product conformity status, trade lane, customs procedure, facility or operating model can require a fresh regulatory and operational assessment.

Core Outcome

A properly organised and lawfully compliant logistics or supply chain operation in Saudi Arabia — spanning freight transport, customs clearance, freight brokerage, forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics, port or air-cargo distribution, GCC trade or 3PL activity — supported by the relevant commercial registration, TGA licence, Fasah account, customs broker authority, product approvals and commercial contracts.

Object Definition

Logistics and supply chain services in Saudi Arabia form the commercial function concerned with the planning, physical movement, storage, customs clearance and coordination of goods across domestic, GCC and international networks, and with determining which commercial registrations, transport licences, customs registrations, product approvals and compliance measures apply to a given service role, goods category, facility or trade lane. The function is broader than moving a shipment: it connects freight transport, brokerage and forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics, 3PL coordination, customs classification, Fasah declarations, transport documentation, product conformity, liability rules and ongoing regulatory supervision.

DefinitionThe commercial and regulatory function covering logistics coordination, freight transport, freight brokerage and forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics, 3PL services and customs clearance for goods moving into, out of or within Saudi Arabia.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of the logistics and supply chain function is to ensure that the movement, storage, forwarding, customs clearance and distribution of goods are conducted reliably, lawfully and to commercial expectations, by entities holding the commercial registration, transport licence, Fasah status, customs authority, product approvals and operational capacity required for the selected service model. The function establishes how goods flow through the supply chain, which party is responsible for each stage, and what Saudi transport, customs, VAT, conformity, reporting and liability obligations apply.

PurposeTo ensure reliable, lawful and properly documented planning, movement, storage, forwarding, customs clearance and distribution of goods within, into, out of or through Saudi Arabia.
Primary OutcomeA functioning logistics or supply chain arrangement — supported, where relevant, by an appropriate commercial registration and activity, TGA/Logisti licence, Fasah importer/exporter registration, customs broker authority, customs procedure, SABER/SASO or other product approval, warehouse arrangement or service agreement — that defines the operational and legal position of the importer, exporter, carrier, freight broker, forwarder, customs broker, warehouse operator, 3PL provider or trader.
Business ValueWell-structured logistics arrangements and early regulatory clarity can reduce cargo delay, customs and VAT errors, product conformity holds, contractual liability exposure, working-capital disruption and later enforcement or audit risk.

Country Characteristics

Saudi Arabia's logistics environment is shaped by continental scale, GCC location, Red Sea and Gulf maritime access, land corridors, energy and industrial supply chains, a growing e-commerce and warehousing market, and a policy emphasis on digital trade and transport regulation. The operational classification of the service is central: carrier, freight broker, logistics provider, customs broker, importer/exporter, warehouse operator, bonded facility and 3PL provider may have different commercial registration, TGA, Fasah, ZATCA, municipal, port/airport and product-control requirements. Fasah is the central customs platform, while Logisti is the TGA licensing route for relevant transport and logistics activities.

Operational CultureDigitally administered, trade-document intensive, institutionally structured and goods-specific. Commercial registration, licensed activity, Fasah, Customs, product conformity, warehouse, port/airport, carrier and client data must remain consistent across Saudi systems.
Institutional StructureTGA regulates and licenses land, rail and maritime transport activities through Logisti. ZATCA administers customs, VAT/Zakat and Fasah. The Ministry of Commerce and Saudi Business Center administer commercial registration. SASO/SABER, SFDA, municipalities, ports, airports and other agencies apply product, facility and modal requirements.
Classification LogicThe initial classification distinguishes commercial registration activity, transport service type, carrier versus freight broker/forwarder role, importer/exporter status, customs broker authority, goods type and facility. The next classification determines Fasah, customs declaration, product conformity, duty/VAT, Customs warehouse, port/airport and GCC road-freight requirements.
Language ExpectationArabic is central to government filings, commercial registration and legal instruments. English is extensively used in international logistics, ports, airports, freight forwarding, technical documentation and commercial contracts. Formal filings and approvals should use the language, data fields and terminology required by the competent Saudi authority.

Applicable Legislation

Saudi Arabia does not apply the EU Community Licence or Union Customs Code framework. Its logistics and supply chain function is governed by Saudi transport, customs, commercial registration, VAT/Zakat, product conformity, port/airport, facility and product-control law. The central classification questions are the actual business activity, commercial registration, transport service role, importer/exporter status, customs broker delegation, goods category, product conformity, Customs procedure, facility and trade lane. Freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics and 3PL arrangements are commercial functions but commonly connect to regulated transport, Customs, product, municipal and facility requirements.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

There is no single universal Saudi logistics process because the correct route depends on the service role, entity, commercial registration, goods, transport mode, facility, port/airport, customs procedure and trade lane. Nevertheless, most operations move from entity and activity classification into commercial registration and TGA licensing, Fasah/Cus­toms setup, product and facility preparation, customs declaration, release or delivery, and ongoing compliance with transport, Customs, VAT and service conditions.

1. Define the OperationIdentify whether the undertaking acts as carrier, freight broker, forwarder, customs broker, importer, exporter, warehouse operator, 3PL provider or trader; define the goods, facility, port/airport/road interface, Customs status, GCC/international trade lane and service model.
2. Confirm Commercial Registration and ActivityConfirm the Saudi legal entity, commercial registration, business activity, authorised persons, municipality/facility requirements, ownership and actual operating capacity. The activity stated on the commercial registration should cover the proposed transport, logistics, trading, warehousing or forwarding function.
3. Screen Regulatory TriggersAssess TGA/Logisti licensing, road freight and carrier/broker requirements, Fasah importer/exporter registration, customs broker authorisation, Customs procedure, duty/VAT, product conformity, food/pharmaceutical controls, Customs warehouse, port/airport and other relevant regimes.
4. Identify the Competent Authority or CounterpartyDetermine whether the matter falls to TGA, ZATCA/Fasah, the Ministry of Commerce, Saudi Business Center, SASO/SABER, SFDA, Mawani, GACA, municipality, a customs broker, forwarder, warehouse or 3PL provider.
5. Establish Transport, Customs and Facility AccessApply for relevant TGA licences through Logisti; register on Fasah as importer/exporter; obtain or confirm customs data; appoint and authorise a licensed customs broker where appropriate; establish warehouse, municipal, port, airport, bonded or product-control arrangements.
6. Prepare the Documentation BaseDevelop commercial registration, licence, invoice, packing list, bill of lading/air waybill/road transport document, certificate of origin, tariff classification, Customs value, Fasah declaration, product conformity, warehouse/3PL and freight-forwarding agreements.
7. Submit Declarations and Obtain ClearancesSubmit Customs information and declaration through Fasah, normally at least 48 hours before cargo arrival when the relevant process applies; obtain product and agency approvals; complete inspection, duty/VAT, guarantee and port/airport procedures.
8. Receive, Store, Distribute or ExportComplete Customs release, move goods to the lawful warehouse or facility, maintain inventory and Customs controls, deliver domestically or through GCC lanes, or export/re-export under the selected procedure and commercial arrangement.
9. Operate, Monitor and Manage ChangeMaintain commercial registration, TGA licence, Fasah, customs broker authority, VAT, product conformity, warehouse, vehicle, goods-control and recordkeeping compliance; reassess before changing activity, goods, facilities, trade lanes, broker/declarant roles or ownership.
Decision logic: First identify the Saudi entity, commercial activity, actual service role and goods/customs status. Then determine whether TGA licensing, Fasah importer/exporter registration, customs broker authorisation, Customs procedure, product conformity, facility and port/airport routes apply. Only after those roles are defined can the correct authority, document package, pre-arrival process and contractual responsibilities be planned reliably.

Timeline

Saudi logistics setup, commercial registration, TGA licensing, Fasah registration, Customs and product conformity preparation should be treated as part of supply-chain design rather than late administrative tasks. TGA licensing begins with the commercial registration and relevant activity; the published TGA investor route identifies commercial registration with logistics services activity, Logisti application, municipality permit, GOSI certificate and ZATCA clearance where applicable. For imports, ZATCA asks importers to complete Fasah clearance procedures and customs declaration data at least 48 hours before cargo arrives at the port of entry.

Cross-Border Relevance

Logistics and supply chain operations in Saudi Arabia are inherently cross-border. Saudi Arabia connects the GCC, Red Sea, Arabian Gulf, Middle East, Africa, Asia and global shipping and air-cargo networks. It is not an EU or EORI jurisdiction; Saudi commercial registration, TGA licences, Fasah accounts, ZATCA Customs declarations and product conformity are distinct from EU Community Licence, VAT and EORI processes. The same goods flow can require Saudi commercial registration, Fasah Customs clearance, GCC customs duty/VAT assessment, port/airport handling, road freight documentation and separate origin/destination jurisdiction requirements.

Foreign CompaniesA foreign group intending to directly conduct Saudi import/export, logistics or distribution activity should determine whether a suitable Saudi entity, commercial registration, licensed carrier, freight broker, customs broker, importer/exporter, warehouse or 3PL provider performs each regulated function. The commercial registration, Fasah, Customs, VAT, product conformity, facility, contract and goods-control roles should be aligned before operations start.
Saudi Arabia-EU and International ContextSaudi Arabia does not use EU Community Licences or EORI numbers. Saudi transport licensing is administered through TGA; importer/exporter and Customs procedures are managed through ZATCA and Fasah. EU-side transport, EORI, VAT, Customs and product rules remain separate for goods moving into or through EU jurisdictions.
GCC ContextSaudi Arabia applies the GCC customs framework in its Customs environment. The commercial and Customs route should distinguish Saudi domestic release, GCC movement, transit, re-export, free-zone or Customs warehouse status, and the importer's duty/VAT responsibility for the particular goods and route.
Customs and Fasah ContextFasah is the electronic route for importer/exporter registration, Customs declarations and customs broker authorisation. A trader uses its commercial registration or ID number to create the account. Customs declaration data and required documents should be completed at least 48 hours before cargo arrival where the published ZATCA import instruction applies.
Product Conformity ContextCustoms clearance can be dependent on product-specific approvals. Technical products may require SASO/SABER conformity procedures, while food, medicines, medical devices, animals, plants, chemicals, telecommunications and strategic goods can have separate agency routes. The Customs declaration must be coordinated with the correct product approval.
Language ConsiderationsArabic is central to government and legal procedures; English is extensively used in international transport, ports, airports and commercial logistics. Cross-border documents must also meet origin/destination country certificate-of-origin, labelling, Customs, product conformity and language requirements.
Practical RiskAssuming that a Saudi commercial registration alone creates Customs importing authority, that a Fasah account replaces product conformity or TGA licensing, or that a global freight forwarder or customs broker automatically assumes the Saudi importer, Customs, VAT, facility and liability role without expressly documented authority.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central practical risk is treating Saudi logistics, commercial registration, TGA licensing, Fasah, Customs, VAT, product conformity and commercial contracts as separate administrative tasks rather than a single integrated operating model. Errors in entity activity, service role, customs broker authority, Customs declaration, product compliance, warehouse status or contract allocation can affect cargo release, cost, working capital and the ability to operate as planned.

Commercial Registration and Activity RiskThe entity's commercial registration and activity may not cover the actual logistics, carrier, freight brokerage, forwarding, warehousing, customs or import/export function. A change in operational scope can require a registration amendment or additional approval before activity begins.
TGA Licence and Transport Document RiskA carrier or freight broker may conduct a regulated land transport or logistics activity without the correct TGA licence, Logisti registration, vehicle documents, municipal/facility approvals or goods transport documentation. Licence scope and conditions should be reviewed before fleet or route expansion.
Fasah and Customs Broker Role RiskThe importer/exporter, Fasah account holder, Customs declarant, customs broker, freight forwarder, carrier, warehouse and 3PL provider may be different parties. Unclear broker authorisation, data ownership, Customs representation, duty/VAT liability and document control can create clearance and contractual risk.
Pre-Arrival and Customs Timing RiskZATCA asks importers to complete Fasah clearance procedures and the Customs declaration at least 48 hours before cargo arrival where the import instruction applies. Late, incomplete or inconsistent declaration data can delay inspection, duty/VAT assessment, release and delivery.
Product Conformity and Goods Control RiskIncorrect HS classification, Customs value, origin, product description, SASO/SABER certificate, SFDA or other agency approval, licence or procedure selection can affect duty, VAT, release timing, product compliance and Customs audit exposure.
Warehouse, Port and GCC Route RiskUnclear allocation of goods control, Customs status, inventory responsibility, duty/VAT, port/airport interfaces, inland transport, GCC transit and liability between importer, broker, forwarder, carrier, warehouse and 3PL provider can create operational and contractual disputes.
Cost DriversCommercial registration and TGA licence fees, municipal/facility requirements, customs duty and 15% import VAT, Customs broker and Fasah costs, product conformity certificates, port/airport/terminal charges, warehouse and 3PL fees, road freight and fuel, IT systems, professional advisers and potential penalties, storage or demurrage.

FAQ

What must a business have before registering as an importer or exporter in Saudi Arabia?The trader registers through the Fasah platform using its commercial registration number or ID number, completes the required information and activates the account. The commercial registration should support the intended trade activity. The trader can then use Fasah Customs services and, where appropriate, authorise a licensed customs broker.
What is Fasah?Fasah is Saudi Arabia's Customs and trade electronic platform. It supports importer/exporter registration, Customs declarations, customs broker authorisation, shipment tracking, Customs procedures and associated trade-document services administered through ZATCA.
Can an importer authorise a customs broker through Fasah?Yes. Fasah allows an importer or exporter to authorise a licensed customs broker to undertake Customs procedures on the trader's behalf. The authorisation can cover import only, export only or both, and can be configured for one or several uses as selected by the trader.
What documents are required before goods are imported into Saudi Arabia?ZATCA identifies the commercial invoice, bill of lading and certificate of origin as core import documents. Additional documents depend on the goods, such as product conformity certificates, product registrations, licences, permits or agency clearances. The customs declaration and necessary Fasah data should be completed before arrival according to the applicable procedure.
When must Customs declaration information be submitted for an import shipment?ZATCA's import instructions state that importers should complete Fasah clearing procedures, provide necessary documents and complete the customs declaration form at least 48 hours before the shipment arrives at the port of entry. The importer may submit directly or use an authorised customs broker when the documents are available.
Do freight forwarding and warehousing require the same licence in Saudi Arabia?No. The applicable route depends on the actual commercial activity, transport mode, commercial registration, TGA licensing category, facility, Customs status and product type. Freight transport, freight brokerage, customs clearance, warehousing, customs-controlled storage, contract logistics and 3PL can each require different licences, permits, trade licence activities, facility approvals, Customs registrations or contractual arrangements.
