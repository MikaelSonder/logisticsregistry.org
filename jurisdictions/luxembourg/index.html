Logistics and supply chain services in Luxembourg cover the commercial function of planning, moving, storing, clearing and coordinating goods across road, rail, inland waterway, sea-linked, air and multimodal networks. The function includes freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics, third-party logistics (3PL), distribution, customs handling and carrier coordination, together with the licensing and compliance layers that determine whether an operator may lawfully perform regulated transport activity.

Luxembourg's small but highly international economy, central location between Belgium, France and Germany, direct access to the Moselle inland waterway, rail and road corridors, and Luxembourg Airport air-cargo function make cross-border, air, road, rail, inland-waterway and multimodal logistics central to the professional function. To operate as a goods carrier, an undertaking must first obtain an authorisation of establishment for the regulated occupation of road haulage operator from the Ministry of the Economy. It then applies for a Community Licence from the Department of Mobility and Transport in the Ministry of Mobility and Public Works. The original Community Licence is retained at the company headquarters and certified true copies are issued for individual vehicles.

For trade with countries outside the EU, the Luxembourg Customs and Excise Administration (ADA) administers EORI identity, import, export, transit, tariff classification, customs procedures and customs authorisations under the Union Customs Code. An EORI number is mandatory for customs declarations. In Luxembourg, the EORI number is identical to the VAT number: LU followed by eight digits. Luxembourg's Single Window for Logistics provides an institutional information route covering customs prerequisites, logistics formalities and related trader procedures.

For international businesses, the Luxembourg logistics environment should be assessed early alongside supply chain design, airport, rail, inland-waterway or warehouse selection, freight procurement, carrier and 3PL contracting, customs strategy and business-establishment requirements. An establishment authorisation, Community Licence or EORI identity does not replace other approvals, and a change in fleet, vehicle category, goods type, trade lane, customs procedure or operating model can require a fresh regulatory and operational assessment.

Object Definition

Logistics and supply chain services in Luxembourg form the commercial function concerned with the planning, physical movement, storage, customs clearance and coordination of goods for hire or reward, and with determining which authorisations, licences, registrations and compliance measures apply to a given transport mode, service type, trade lane or goods category. The function is broader than moving a shipment: it connects freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics, 3PL coordination, air-cargo and inland-waterway planning, carrier licensing, customs classification, transport documentation, liability rules and ongoing regulatory supervision.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of the logistics and supply chain function is to ensure that the movement, storage, forwarding and customs clearance of goods are conducted reliably, lawfully and to commercial expectations, by operators who meet applicable establishment, repute, financial and professional standards where regulation applies. The function establishes how goods flow through the supply chain, which party is responsible for each stage, and what documentation, reporting and liability obligations apply.

PurposeTo ensure reliable, lawful and properly documented planning, movement, storage, forwarding and customs clearance of goods within, into and out of Luxembourg.

Country Characteristics

Luxembourg's logistics environment is shaped by its very small domestic market, central Benelux/Central European position, international service economy, land borders with Belgium, France and Germany, Luxembourg Airport, Moselle inland-waterway connection and EU Customs Union membership. The domestic professional road-haulage route is sequential: the undertaking first receives a business permit or authorisation of establishment from the Ministry of the Economy, then applies to the Department of Mobility and Transport for the Community Licence. The Customs and Excise Administration administers customs identification through an EORI that is identical to the Luxembourg VAT number.

Operational CultureInternationally oriented, documentation-led and institutionally coordinated. Carrier, establishment, VAT/EORI, customs, airport, terminal, warehouse and client data must remain consistent across Luxembourg, EU and counterpart-jurisdiction compliance layers.
Institutional StructureThe Ministry of the Economy grants the professional authorisation of establishment. The Department of Mobility and Transport in the Ministry of Mobility and Public Works grants Community Licences and international road transport authorisations. ADA administers EORI and customs. The Department of Inland Navigation and Logistics is relevant to inland-waterway and logistics policy matters.
Classification LogicCommercial road haulage requires prior authorisation of establishment for the regulated road-haulage profession. An international carrier then obtains the Community Licence for eligible EEA and Swiss freight transport. Third-country routes can require bi- or multilateral authorisations and non-EU driver arrangements can require driver attestations. Customs treatment depends on tariff classification, origin, value and EU/non-EU trade status.
Language ExpectationFrench, German and Luxembourgish are material national administrative languages. French is particularly common in formal regulatory and customs interaction. English is widely used in international logistics and air-cargo business, but formal applications and commercial contracts should use terminology appropriate to the competent authority and relationship.

Applicable Legislation

EU Regulations 1071/2009 and 1072/2009 form the principal framework for access to the road-haulage profession and the international road-haulage market. The Union Customs Code governs customs treatment throughout the EU, including Luxembourg. Luxembourg's establishment-law framework provides the prerequisite business permit for road haulage, after which the Community Licence is granted through the transport licensing desk. Freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics and 3PL arrangements are generally organised through commercial contract and general Luxembourg commercial law, unless a connected activity is separately regulated.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

There is no single universal logistics process because the appropriate route depends on service type, transport mode, vehicle category and trade lane. Nevertheless, most Luxembourg operations move from planning and classification into company and establishment authorisation, preparation of operational and financial material, formal Community Licence, customs or contracting setup, review, decision and ongoing compliance with licence conditions, customs obligations or service agreements.

1. Define the OperationIdentify the service type (transport, forwarding, warehousing, fulfilment, contract logistics, 3PL), transport mode, vehicle category, goods type, Luxembourg establishment, airport, Moselle or terminal interface, trade lane and whether movement is domestic, intra-EU, EEA/Swiss or third-country.
2. Screen Legal TriggersAssess the activity against Luxembourg authorisation-of-establishment requirements, Community Licence and cabotage rules, bilateral/multilateral permit needs, EORI and Union Customs Code requirements, air/inland navigation rules and other relevant regimes.
3. Identify the Competent Authority or CounterpartyDetermine whether the matter falls to the Ministry of the Economy, Department of Mobility and Transport, ADA Customs, an airport, inland navigation or terminal authority, a warehouse or 3PL provider, or another party.
4. Obtain Establishment Authorisation and Prepare EvidenceSecure the authorisation of establishment for the road-haulage profession; assemble professional competence, good-repute, financial-standing, vehicle and corporate evidence; confirm Luxembourg VAT/EORI identity and customs readiness where relevant.
5. Prepare the Documentation BaseDevelop Community Licence, certified-copy, driver-attestation or international permit applications, transport and warehousing contracts, tariff-classification records, customs declarations and proposed operating conditions.
6. Submit, Contract and CompleteAfter obtaining the establishment authorisation, submit the original Community Licence application to the Department of Mobility and Transport Licensing Desk; complete EORI/customs activity with ADA; finalise forwarding or warehousing agreements; address requests for clarification or supplementary material.
7. ExaminationThe authority or counterparty reviews the material, may request further evidence, and assesses professional access, establishment, good repute, financial standing, customs classification or service terms.
8. Decision and ConditionsAn establishment authorisation, Community Licence, certified vehicle copy, driver attestation, international permit, VAT/EORI identity, customs authorisation or service agreement may set scope, validity, reporting duties, cabotage limits and other operating conditions.
9. Operate, Monitor and Manage ChangeMaintain compliance, retain the Community Licence original at the headquarters and certified copies in vehicles as required, meet customs and reporting duties, and assess whether fleet, service scope, trade lane or goods-category changes require renewal, amendment or a new process.
Decision logic: First identify the service type, transport mode, vehicle category, Luxembourg establishment and trade lane. Then determine whether the undertaking needs the authorisation of establishment, Community Licence, international permit, customs and contractual route. Only after the professional-access route is confirmed can the competent authority, documentation package and compliance timeline be reliably planned.

The Luxembourg carrier route is sequential: a business permit is obtained first, followed by the Community Licence application. Timing depends on completeness of establishment, professional, financial and corporate evidence, authority processing, VAT/EORI and customs readiness, and the complexity of airport, terminal or trade-lane interfaces.

Cross-Border Relevance

Logistics and supply chain operations in Luxembourg are inherently cross-border. Luxembourg is fully integrated with Benelux, French and German freight networks and serves international air, road, rail and inland-waterway operations. It participates in the EU single market and Customs Union. The distinctive two-stage professional access route — national establishment authorisation followed by Community Licence — must be managed together with EU customs identity, air-cargo and terminal responsibilities. Foreign investors, group companies, carriers, forwarders, warehouse operators, customs representatives and 3PL providers need clarity on which entity holds each authorisation, licence, EORI identity and contractual responsibility.

Foreign CompaniesA foreign-owned undertaking established in Luxembourg is generally assessed under the same Luxembourg and EU framework as a domestic operator. A genuine Luxembourg establishment and authorisation of establishment are prerequisites for the local road-haulage professional route, before the Community Licence application is filed.
EU and EEA ContextA Luxembourg Community Licence supports eligible international road haulage in the EU/EEA and Switzerland, subject to cabotage, driver, vehicle and other applicable rules. For countries outside this area, a bilateral or multilateral authorisation may be required in addition to the Community Licence.
Air, Moselle and Central Location ContextLuxembourg Airport, the Moselle waterway, rail terminals and immediate links to Belgium, France and Germany make multimodal interfaces highly material. The allocation of importer, declarant, carrier, terminal, warehouse and 3PL responsibility should be explicit.
Customs ContextLuxembourg is part of the EU Customs Union. The Luxembourg EORI is identical to the VAT number, using LU plus eight digits, and is mandatory for customs declarations. It is valid throughout the EU. Goods entering or leaving the EU customs territory require the applicable customs procedure and supporting documentation.
Language ConsiderationsFrench, German and Luxembourgish are relevant in national administration. English is widely used in international air-cargo, financial and logistics contexts. Formal applications, customs records and contracts should use language and terminology appropriate to the competent authority and counterparties.
Practical RiskAssuming that a Community Licence can be obtained without prior Luxembourg authorisation of establishment, that the small domestic market reduces cross-border compliance needs, or that VAT/EORI identity eliminates carrier, customs, terminal or contractual responsibility issues without separate assessment.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central practical risk is treating Luxembourg logistics licensing and customs compliance as a one-time filing exercise rather than an ongoing operational and compliance process. Errors in the sequential establishment-authorisation and Community Licence route, vehicle-copy control, VAT/EORI use, customs or service documentation and insufficient attention to airport, terminal, carrier and contractual conditions can affect timing, cost and the ability to operate as planned.

Sequencing RiskA carrier may seek international Community Licence access before it has obtained the prerequisite Luxembourg authorisation of establishment. The two approvals have distinct authorities, documentation and operational purposes.
Licence Original and Vehicle Copy RiskThe original Community Licence must be kept at the company headquarters and certified true copies must be available in the vehicles. Fleet additions, substitutions or document-control failures can create compliance exposure.
Professional Access RiskThe undertaking must maintain the authorisation-of-establishment conditions, including a suitable technical manager, good repute, professional competence, financial standing and genuine Luxembourg operating position.
Customs and VAT/EORI RiskIncorrect tariff classification, customs value, origin treatment, LU VAT/EORI data or procedure selection can affect duty, VAT, release timing and customs audit exposure. EORI must be in place before customs declarations are filed.
International Permit and Driver RiskThird-country routes may require bi- or multilateral authorisations, and non-EU drivers may require driver attestations. Cabotage, working-time, vehicle and route rules create additional operating controls.
Cost DriversEstablishment-authorisation and licence fees, professional and financial evidence, certified vehicle copies, customs duties and import VAT, air-cargo, terminal and warehouse charges, 3PL fees, IT and declaration systems, professional advisers and possible penalties.

What does a Luxembourg Community Licence allow?The Community Licence enables carriers to carry goods by road for hire or reward throughout the EEA and Switzerland, subject to applicable EU rules. When are additional international road transport authorisations needed?For transport to or from countries outside the EEA or Switzerland, a bi- or multilateral authorisation may be required in addition to the Community Licence unless a liberalisation agreement applies. What is the EORI number in Luxembourg?Luxembourg's EORI number is identical to the company's VAT number: LU followed by eight digits. It is mandatory for customs declarations and is valid throughout the EU customs territory.
