Logistics and supply chain services in Japan cover the commercial function of planning, moving, storing, clearing and coordinating goods across road, rail, coastal sea, international maritime, air and multimodal networks. The function includes freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics, third-party logistics (3PL), distribution, customs handling and carrier coordination, together with the registration, permission, licensing and compliance layers that determine whether an operator may lawfully perform regulated logistics activity.

Japan's island geography, large domestic market, globally connected ports and airports, dense domestic road and rail networks, high-value manufacturing and extensive import/export activity make maritime, air, coastal, road and warehouse logistics central to the professional function. Domestic road freight is regulated under the Motor Truck Transportation Business Act. General motor truck transportation business requires permission from the Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (MLIT) through the competent District Transport Bureau. Freight forwarding is separately regulated under the Consigned Freight Forwarding Business Act: first-class consigned freight forwarding businesses are registered, while second-class businesses — including certain transport modes and integrated arrangements — require MLIT permission.

For international trade, Japan Customs administers import, export, transit, tariff classification, bonded warehousing and customs clearance under the Customs Act. Import and export declarations may be submitted to Customs through NACCS, the Nippon Automated Cargo and Port Consolidated System. Corporations normally use their Corporate Number as the importer/exporter code in customs declarations; individuals or sole proprietorships without a Corporate Number use a Customs-issued importer/exporter code or a code issued by the Japan Association for Simplification of International Trade Procedures (JASTPRO). Japan does not use the EU EORI system.

For international businesses, the Japanese logistics environment should be assessed early alongside supply chain design, port, airport or warehouse selection, freight procurement, carrier and 3PL contracting, customs strategy, importer-of-record arrangements and Japanese-language regulatory administration. A motor truck permit, freight-forwarding registration or permission, bonded warehouse licence or importer/exporter identifier does not replace other approvals, and a change in transport mode, service model, goods category, trade lane or operating structure can require a fresh regulatory and operational assessment.

Object Definition

Logistics and supply chain services in Japan form the commercial function concerned with the planning, physical movement, storage, customs clearance and coordination of goods for hire or reward, and with determining which permissions, registrations and compliance measures apply to a given transport mode, service type, trade lane or goods category. The function is broader than moving a shipment: it connects motor truck transport, freight forwarding, warehousing, bonded storage, 3PL coordination, domestic and international multimodal planning, customs classification, NACCS declarations, transport documentation, liability rules and ongoing regulatory supervision.

DefinitionThe commercial and regulatory function covering logistics coordination, freight transport, freight forwarding, warehousing, bonded warehousing, 3PL services and customs clearance for goods moving into, out of or within Japan.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of the logistics and supply chain function is to ensure that the movement, storage, forwarding and customs clearance of goods are conducted reliably, lawfully and to commercial expectations, by operators holding the permissions, registrations, licences and professional capacity required for the service model. The function establishes how goods flow through the supply chain, which party is responsible for each stage, and what Japanese customs, reporting, documentary and liability obligations apply.

PurposeTo ensure reliable, lawful and properly documented planning, movement, storage, forwarding, bonded storage and customs clearance of goods within, into and out of Japan.
Primary OutcomeA functioning logistics or supply chain arrangement — supported, where relevant, by motor truck transport permission, first-class registration or second-class forwarding permission, bonded warehouse licence, NACCS access, importer/exporter identifier, customs procedure or warehousing agreement — that defines the operational and legal position of the carrier, forwarder, warehouse operator, 3PL provider, customs broker or trader.
Business ValueWell-structured logistics arrangements and early regulatory clarity can reduce customs delay, port congestion exposure, compliance errors, contractual liability exposure, working-capital disruption and later enforcement or audit risk.

Request Contexts, Users and Scenarios

Logistics and supply chain work is normally activated by a new Japanese trade lane, domestic distribution model, warehouse requirement, port or airport interface, or entry into freight, forwarding or customs activity. The initial question is not simply whether a permit is required, but whether the undertaking acts as carrier, first-class forwarder, second-class forwarder, warehouse operator, customs broker, importer/exporter or 3PL provider, and whether the proposed activity changes the legal scope of an existing registration or commercial arrangement.

Typical UserMotor truck carriers, freight forwarders, customs brokers, warehouse, fulfilment and 3PL operators, contract-logistics providers, port and airport logistics participants, manufacturers with in-house logistics, e-commerce importers, life-sciences and high-value goods supply-chain participants, investors and foreign companies establishing Japanese freight or distribution operations.
Business EventNew road freight operation, Japanese import/export trade lane, port or airport warehouse establishment, 3PL contracting, motor truck transport permission, first-class registration or second-class forwarding permission, bonded warehouse arrangement, new product import/export line, customs procedure change or acquisition of a regulated logistics business.
Typical ScenarioA company plans paid domestic road carriage and must obtain general motor truck transportation business permission; a logistics provider arranges freight on behalf of customers and must distinguish first-class registration from second-class forwarding permission; an importer uses its Corporate Number or Customs-issued importer/exporter code in a NACCS declaration; a manufacturer appoints a 3PL provider for Japanese distribution and must allocate warehousing, customs and delivery responsibilities.
Professional AssistanceTypically relevant when the carrier/forwarder/warehouse role is uncertain, customs classification is complex, bonded storage or NACCS access is needed, supply chain design spans several modes or jurisdictions, or product-specific import and export controls are relevant.

Country Characteristics

Japan's logistics environment is shaped by island geography, high population density in major metropolitan areas, strong domestic transport networks, globally significant ports and airports, a high-value manufacturing base and detailed sectoral administration. The legal classification of the operator's service is central: road carrier, freight forwarding business, warehousing business, bonded warehouse operator, customs broker and importer/exporter each have distinct regulatory and operational functions. NACCS provides the core electronic platform through which customs, port and airport procedures are processed with customs, other government authorities and private-sector participants.

Operational CultureDocumentation-led, quality-sensitive, digitally administered and service-role specific. Japanese-language operational records, customs data, cargo documentation, facility controls and counterpart responsibilities must remain consistent across transport, port, airport, warehouse and customs layers.
Institutional StructureMLIT and its District Transport Bureaus administer transport, forwarding and warehousing business permissions or registrations. Japan Customs administers customs and bonded warehouse licensing. NACCS Center operates the digital platform for customs, port, airport and trade-related procedures. JASTPRO supports importer/exporter identification in appropriate cases.
Classification LogicGeneral motor truck transportation requires MLIT permission. First-class consigned freight forwarding requires registration, while second-class consigned freight forwarding requires MLIT permission. A bonded warehouse requires Customs Director-General licensing. Importers/exporters use a Corporate Number, Customs-issued code or JASTPRO code in NACCS as appropriate. Customs treatment depends on tariff classification, origin, value, goods controls and the selected customs procedure.
Language ExpectationJapanese is central to permits, registrations, customs filings, authority correspondence and many operational documents. English guidance is available from Japan Customs and selected MLIT sources, but formal filings, contracts and compliance records should use precise Japanese terminology or professionally controlled translations.

Applicable Legislation

Japan does not apply the EU Community Licence or Union Customs Code framework. Its logistics function is governed by national transport, freight-forwarding, warehousing and customs legislation, administered through MLIT and Japan Customs. The principal classification question is the actual service role and transport mode: carrier, first-class forwarder, second-class forwarder, warehouse operator, bonded warehouse operator, customs broker or importer/exporter. Freight forwarding, warehousing and 3PL arrangements are also shaped by commercial contract, but the regulated activity must be identified before the commercial arrangement is finalised.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

There is no single universal logistics process because the correct route depends on the service role, transport mode, goods category, facility and trade lane. Nevertheless, most Japanese operations move from classification and supply-chain design into corporate and facility preparation, transport or forwarding permission/registration, customs and NACCS setup, contracting, authority review and ongoing compliance with the applicable business, customs and service conditions.

1. Define the OperationIdentify whether the undertaking acts as carrier, first-class or second-class forwarder, warehouse or bonded warehouse operator, customs broker, importer/exporter or 3PL provider; define the transport mode, goods, facility, port/airport interface and trade lane.
2. Screen Legal TriggersAssess the activity against the Motor Truck Transportation Business Act, Consigned Freight Forwarding Business Act, Warehousing Business Act, Customs Act, product-specific import/export controls and other relevant regimes.
3. Identify the Competent Authority or CounterpartyDetermine whether the matter falls to MLIT, the competent District Transport Bureau, Japan Customs, NACCS Center, a port or airport authority, a customs broker, a warehouse or 3PL provider, or another party.
4. Establish Corporate and Operating BaseConfirm Japanese company or branch structure, registered office, operating facilities, responsible personnel, vehicles or transport contracts, warehouse rights and operational capacity.
5. Prepare the Documentation BaseDevelop transport-permission, forwarding registration or permission applications, warehouse documents, customs identifiers, NACCS setup, tariff-classification records, declarations, contracts and proposed operating procedures.
6. Submit, Register, Contract and CompleteFile the appropriate MLIT or District Transport Bureau application, Customs/bonded warehouse request or NACCS setup; obtain Corporate Number or importer/exporter code status as relevant; finalise forwarding, warehousing and 3PL agreements.
7. ExaminationThe authority or counterparty reviews the material, may request further evidence, and assesses business scope, facilities, responsible persons, customs classification, bonded controls or service terms.
8. Decision and ConditionsA transport permission, forwarding registration or permission, warehouse registration, bonded warehouse licence, NACCS access, customs procedure or service agreement may set scope, validity, facility, reporting and other operating conditions.
9. Operate, Monitor and Manage ChangeMaintain compliance, lodge customs declarations through the required route, manage bonded or warehouse records, and assess whether changes in mode, facility, business scope, goods or trade lanes require an amendment, new permission or new registration.
Decision logic: First identify the undertaking's actual service role and transport mode. Then determine whether the activity is carrier transport, first-class or second-class freight forwarding, warehousing, bonded storage, customs representation or importer/exporter activity. Only after the legal role is identified can the correct Japanese authority, document package, electronic customs setup and operating timeline be reliably planned.

Timeline

Japanese logistics permissions, forwarding registration, customs setup and facility approvals should be treated as part of supply chain design rather than a late administrative step. Timing depends on the service classification, corporate establishment, vehicle or facility readiness, completeness of Japanese-language documentation, the relevant District Transport Bureau or Customs office, NACCS access, the goods category and the selected port or airport. Bonded warehouse and product-controlled-goods activity can require additional sequencing.

Cross-Border Relevance

Logistics and supply chain operations in Japan are inherently cross-border because the country is an island economy with globally integrated manufacturing, retail and import/export supply chains. Japan is outside the EU, EEA and EU Customs Union, so its domestic transport permissions, importer/exporter code structure, NACCS procedures and Customs Act framework are distinct from EU Community Licence and EORI systems. Foreign investors, group companies, carriers, forwarders, warehouse operators, customs brokers and 3PL providers need clarity on which Japanese entity performs the regulated function, acts as importer/exporter, controls the goods and bears customs and contractual responsibility.

Foreign CompaniesA foreign-owned undertaking operating in Japan must identify whether it has a Japanese entity, branch, appointed customs representative, licensed carrier, registered forwarder or contracted 3PL provider performing each regulated function. The legal role, Japanese establishment and actual operational control must align with the applicable permission, registration and customs process.
Japan-EU and International ContextJapan does not use the EU Community Licence or EORI system. Japanese road transport, freight forwarding and customs activity are governed by domestic permissions and identifiers. EU-side road transport and customs requirements remain separate for goods moving into or through EU jurisdictions.
Importer/Exporter IdentificationJapanese corporations generally use their Corporate Number in import and export declaration fields. Individuals or sole proprietorships without a Corporate Number use a Customs-issued importer/exporter code or JASTPRO code. The selected identifier should be aligned with the party declared in NACCS and the relevant customs role.
Customs and NACCS ContextJapan Customs accepts import and export declarations through NACCS. NACCS connects Customs, other government authorities and related private-sector participants for ships, aircraft and import/export cargo. The customs process must be designed alongside the commercial allocation of importer, exporter, declarant, forwarder and warehouse roles.
Language ConsiderationsJapanese is central to authority interaction, permissions, registrations, customs communication and many commercial documents. English-language customs materials exist, but international businesses should ensure controlled Japanese versions of key filings, contracts, procedures and goods descriptions.
Practical RiskAssuming that an overseas carrier, EU EORI, foreign forwarding registration or global 3PL contract automatically provides the Japanese transport, forwarding, bonded warehouse, importer/exporter or customs authority needed for the actual operating model.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central practical risk is treating Japanese logistics permissions, customs and commercial contracts as separate administrative tasks rather than a single integrated operating model. Errors in the classification of carrier, forwarding, warehousing, bonded storage, customs and importer/exporter roles; inconsistent Japanese-language documentation; or insufficient attention to NACCS and facility obligations can affect timing, cost and the ability to operate as planned.

Service Classification RiskAn undertaking may misclassify itself as an unregulated arranger or 3PL provider when its actual activity requires motor truck permission, first-class forwarding registration, second-class forwarding permission, warehousing registration, bonded warehouse licence or customs broker involvement.
Facility and Bonded Warehouse RiskA warehouse storing foreign cargo before customs clearance must have the appropriate Customs licence and controls. Ordinary commercial warehousing and bonded storage have different legal and documentary profiles.
Customs Identifier and NACCS RiskUsing an incorrect importer/exporter code, Corporate Number, declarant role or NACCS data can interrupt electronic customs processing. The identifier and responsible party must be consistent across the customs declaration, contract and operational record.
Customs and Product Classification RiskIncorrect tariff classification, customs value, origin treatment, goods description, product-control evidence or customs procedure selection can affect duty, consumption tax, release timing, product compliance and audit exposure.
Port, Air and Intermodal RiskShipping schedules, airport handling, container or air-cargo handovers, domestic trucking, warehouse capacity and terminal controls can create disruption if responsibilities and data interfaces between parties are unclear.
Cost DriversPermission, registration and professional costs; bonded facility investment; customs duties and consumption tax; port, airport and terminal charges; warehouse and 3PL fees; NACCS and IT systems; Japanese-language documentation; product-control support; professional advisers and potential penalties.

FAQ

Does a Japanese company need permission to operate commercial road freight?Yes. General motor truck transportation business is regulated under the Motor Truck Transportation Business Act and requires permission from MLIT through the competent District Transport Bureau. The exact permission route depends on the service model and type of transport business.
What is the difference between first-class and second-class freight forwarding in Japan?Under the Consigned Freight Forwarding Business Act, a person seeking to conduct a first-class consigned freight forwarding business must register with MLIT. A person seeking to conduct a second-class consigned freight forwarding business must obtain MLIT permission. The appropriate class depends on the actual forwarding arrangement and transport mode, so the classification should be assessed before the business begins.
What is a bonded warehouse in Japan?A bonded warehouse is a place licensed by the Director General of Customs where foreign cargo may be loaded, unloaded, transported or stored under customs control. It is distinct from ordinary commercial storage and is relevant where goods are held before import clearance, export or another customs procedure.
Does Japan use an EORI number?No. EORI is an EU customs identifier. In Japanese import and export declarations, corporations normally use their Corporate Number. Persons without a Corporate Number use a Customs-issued importer/exporter code or a JASTPRO code as appropriate. These identifiers are used in NACCS customs declarations.
What is NACCS?NACCS is the Nippon Automated Cargo and Port Consolidated System. It is the electronic platform for online processing of procedures with Japan Customs, other government authorities and related private-sector services for arriving/departing ships and aircraft and import/export cargo.
Are freight forwarding and warehousing separately regulated in Japan?They can be. Freight forwarding is expressly regulated by the Consigned Freight Forwarding Business Act, while warehousing business is regulated separately. Bonded warehousing is licensed by Japan Customs. The correct legal route depends on the actual service role, transport mode, facility and customs status of the goods.
