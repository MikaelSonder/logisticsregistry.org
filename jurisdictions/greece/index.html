Logistics and supply chain services in Greece cover the commercial function of planning, moving, storing, clearing and coordinating goods across road, rail, sea, air and multimodal networks. The function includes freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics, third-party logistics (3PL), distribution, customs handling and carrier coordination, together with the licensing and compliance layers that determine whether an operator may lawfully perform regulated transport activity.

Greece's maritime geography, extensive island network, position at the southeastern edge of the EU, road links to the Balkans, major port infrastructure and connections to the Eastern Mediterranean make sea freight, port logistics, road transport, island distribution and customs material to the professional function. A Greek-established undertaking pursuing the occupation of road haulage operator must obtain an authorisation from the Transport and Communications Department of the Regional Unit where its registered office is located. The authorisation is granted for a maximum of five years. For eligible international freight transport, the undertaking obtains a Community Licence, also valid for five years, with a certified true copy for each motor vehicle engaged in international transport.

For trade with countries outside the EU, the Independent Authority for Public Revenue (AADE) administers EORI identity, import, export, transit, tariff classification, customs procedures and customs authorisations under the Union Customs Code. An EORI is a unique EU customs identifier and permits the operator to use the same identification number in dealings with customs authorities across all EU member states. Applications are submitted or sent by email to the customs authority competent for the applicant's area, with the applicable application-declaration and supporting identification material.

For international businesses, the Greek logistics environment should be assessed early alongside supply chain design, port or warehouse selection, island distribution, freight procurement, carrier and 3PL contracting, customs strategy, Greek establishment and regional authority requirements. A road haulage operator authorisation, Community Licence or EORI registration does not replace other approvals, and a change in fleet, vehicle category, goods type, trade lane, customs procedure or operating model can require a fresh regulatory and operational assessment.

Object Definition

Logistics and supply chain services in Greece form the commercial function concerned with the planning, physical movement, storage, customs clearance and coordination of goods for hire or reward, and with determining which authorisations, licences, registrations and compliance measures apply to a given transport mode, service type, trade lane or goods category. The function is broader than moving a shipment: it connects freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics, 3PL coordination, port and island distribution, carrier authorisation, customs classification, transport documentation, liability rules and ongoing regulatory supervision.

DefinitionThe commercial and regulatory function covering logistics coordination, freight transport, freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics, 3PL services and customs clearance for goods moving into, out of or within Greece.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of the logistics and supply chain function is to ensure that the movement, storage, forwarding and customs clearance of goods are conducted reliably, lawfully and to commercial expectations, by operators who meet applicable establishment, repute, financial and professional standards where regulation applies. The function establishes how goods flow through the supply chain, which party is responsible for each stage, and what documentation, reporting and liability obligations apply.

PurposeTo ensure reliable, lawful and properly documented planning, movement, storage, forwarding and customs clearance of goods within, into and out of Greece.
Primary OutcomeA functioning logistics or supply chain arrangement — supported, where relevant, by a Greek road haulage operator authorisation, Community Licence and certified vehicle copies, EORI identity, customs authorisation or warehousing agreement — that defines the operational and legal position of the carrier, forwarder, warehouse operator, contract-logistics or 3PL provider, or trader.
Business ValueWell-structured logistics arrangements and early regulatory clarity can reduce shipment delay, island or port disruption, customs penalties, contractual liability exposure and later enforcement or audit risk.

Request Contexts, Users and Scenarios

Logistics and supply chain work is normally activated by a new trade lane, fleet expansion, port or warehouse requirement, island distribution model or entry into cross-border freight, forwarding or customs activity. The initial question is not simply whether an authorisation is required, but how the goods flow should be organised, which Regional Unit is competent for the undertaking's registered office, whether the activity falls within a regulated transport or customs category, and whether it changes the legal scope of an existing authorisation or commercial arrangement.

Typical UserRoad-haulage operators, freight forwarders, customs brokers, warehouse, fulfilment and 3PL operators, contract-logistics providers, port, ferry and island logistics participants, manufacturers with in-house logistics, e-commerce importers, investors and foreign companies establishing Greek freight or distribution operations.
Business EventNew haulage fleet, Balkan, Mediterranean or island trade lane, warehouse or fulfilment-centre establishment, 3PL contracting, road-haulage authorisation or Community Licence application, acquisition of a carrier, new import/export product line, customs-warehouse expansion or cabotage operation.
Typical ScenarioA company plans to carry goods for third parties from Greece and must obtain the road haulage operator authorisation from the Transport and Communications Department of the relevant Regional Unit; an international carrier obtains a five-year Community Licence and certified vehicle copies; an importer applies for EORI with the competent customs office; a manufacturer appoints a 3PL provider for Greek mainland and island distribution and must allocate warehouse, customs and delivery responsibilities.
Professional AssistanceTypically relevant when operator-authorisation or Community Licence status is uncertain, customs classification is complex, supply chain design spans island, sea, road, air and several jurisdictions, or driver, cabotage, port and third-country trade rules are relevant.

Country Characteristics

Greece's logistics environment is shaped by its long coastline, large island network, maritime economy, strategic position between the Balkans, Eastern Mediterranean and EU markets, external EU border role and EU Customs Union membership. Road transport authorisation is administered through regional structures: the Transport and Communications Department of the Regional Unit competent for the undertaking's registered office. The same regional administrative route is used for the Community Licence process, with digital availability in specified regions. AADE manages customs and EORI through its national customs system and competent customs authorities.

Operational CultureDocumentation-led, maritime and island-sensitive, and regionally administered. Carrier, vehicle, ferry, port, customs, warehouse and client data must remain consistent across Regional Unit, national, EU and counterpart-jurisdiction layers.
Institutional StructureRegional Units administer road-haulage operator authorisations and Community Licence applications on behalf of the Ministry of Infrastructure and Transport. AADE and competent Customs Authorities administer EORI and customs procedures. Port, coast guard, ferry, airport and island authorities may be relevant to particular operations.
Classification LogicGreek-established transport undertakings pursuing the occupation of road haulage operator require the relevant authorisation, valid for up to five years. A Community Licence, also valid for five years, supports eligible international commercial road freight and requires a certified true copy for each vehicle. Customs treatment depends on tariff classification, origin, value and EU/non-EU trade status.
Language ExpectationGreek is central to Regional Unit transport administration, customs communication and formal filings. English is widely used in international maritime and freight activity, and selected gov.gr and AADE services provide English-language information. Formal licence applications, customs records and contracts should use terminology appropriate to the authority and commercial relationship.

Applicable Legislation

EU Regulations 1071/2009 and 1072/2009 form the principal framework for access to the road-haulage profession and the international road-haulage market. The Union Customs Code governs customs treatment throughout the EU, including Greece. Greek national road transport rules and Regional Unit administrative procedures provide the domestic route for authorisation to engage in the occupation of road haulage operator and Community Licence administration. Freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics and 3PL arrangements are generally organised through commercial contract and general Greek commercial law, unless a connected activity is separately regulated.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

There is no single universal logistics process because the appropriate route depends on service type, transport mode, vehicle category, registered office, island/mainland context and trade lane. Nevertheless, most Greek operations move from planning and classification into company and operator-authorisation preparation, assembly of operational and financial material, formal Community Licence, customs or contracting setup, review, decision and ongoing compliance with transport conditions, customs obligations or service agreements.

1. Define the OperationIdentify the service type (transport, forwarding, warehousing, fulfilment, contract logistics, 3PL), transport mode, vehicle category, goods type, Greek registered office, mainland or island distribution, port or terminal interface, trade lane and whether movement is domestic, intra-EU or third-country.
2. Screen Legal TriggersAssess the activity against Greek road haulage operator authorisation requirements, EU Community Licence and cabotage rules, EORI and Union Customs Code requirements, port/ferry conditions and other relevant regimes.
3. Identify the Competent Authority or CounterpartyDetermine the competent Transport and Communications Department of the Regional Unit and whether the matter also falls to AADE Customs, a port authority, coast guard, warehouse or 3PL provider, or another party.
4. Register and Prepare EvidenceConfirm Greek company establishment; assemble professional competence, good-repute, financial-standing, vehicle and corporate evidence; obtain EORI identity where relevant.
5. Prepare the Documentation BaseDevelop operator-authorisation and Community Licence applications, certified-vehicle-copy material, transport and warehousing contracts, tariff-classification records, customs declarations and proposed operating conditions.
6. Submit, Contract and CompleteFile the operator-authorisation or Community Licence application with the competent Regional Unit; submit the EORI application-declaration to the competent customs authority; finalise the forwarding or warehousing agreement; address requests for clarification or supplementary material.
7. ExaminationThe authority or counterparty reviews the material, may request further evidence, and assesses Greek establishment, good repute, financial standing, professional competence, customs classification or service terms.
8. Decision and ConditionsAn operator authorisation, Community Licence, certified vehicle copy, EORI confirmation, customs authorisation or service agreement may set scope, validity, reporting duties, cabotage limits, maritime interface conditions and other operating requirements.
9. Operate, Monitor and Manage ChangeMaintain compliance, keep certified vehicle copies available where required, meet customs, ferry, port and reporting duties, and assess whether fleet, service scope, registered office, island operation, trade lane or goods-category changes require renewal, amendment or a new process.
Decision logic: First identify the service type, transport mode, vehicle category, Greek registered office, island/mainland operating model and trade lane. Then determine the applicable road haulage authorisation, Community Licence, customs and contractual regime. Only after the operational route is identified can the competent authority or provider, documentation package and compliance timeline be reliably planned.

Timeline

Logistics setup, road haulage operator authorisation and customs preparation should be treated as part of supply chain planning rather than a late administrative step. The published procedure identifies a maximum completion period of 50 days for the Community Licence process where a complete application is received, although individual timing depends on the Regional Unit, completeness of evidence, customs complexity, goods category and island or port interfaces. Operator authorisations and Community Licences have a maximum five-year validity, subject to the validity of supporting evidence where shorter.

Cross-Border Relevance

Logistics and supply chain operations in Greece are inherently cross-border. Greece participates in the EU single market and Customs Union, while its position on the Mediterranean and Balkan routes, external EU border role and island geography create heightened significance for maritime, customs, ferry, port and multimodal planning. Greek road carriers use Community Licences for eligible EU operations, while third-country, island and port-linked supply chains require separate route and customs analysis. Foreign investors, group companies, carriers, forwarders, warehouse operators, customs representatives and 3PL providers need clarity on which entity holds the operator authorisation, Community Licence, vehicle copies, EORI identity and customs responsibilities.

Foreign CompaniesA foreign-owned undertaking established in Greece is generally assessed under the same Greek and EU framework as a domestic operator. The Greek registered office, competent Regional Unit, professional capacity, good repute and financial standing are material to the operator authorisation and Community Licence analysis.
EU ContextA Greek Community Licence supports eligible international road haulage throughout the EU/EEA, subject to cabotage, driver, vehicle and other applicable rules. It is distinct from the domestic operator authorisation and from route-specific third-country or maritime requirements.
Port, Island and Balkan ContextGreek ports, ferry links, islands and Balkan land corridors make sea-road, ferry-road and port-terminal handovers material to many supply chains. The allocation of importer, declarant, carrier, ferry operator, warehouse and 3PL responsibility should be explicit.
Customs ContextGreece is part of the EU Customs Union. A Greek EORI is valid throughout the EU and is used to identify the operator in customs activity. Goods entering or leaving the EU customs territory require the applicable customs procedure and supporting documentation.
Language ConsiderationsGreek is central to Regional Unit and Customs Authority interaction. English is common in international port, shipping and freight activity, while selected gov.gr and AADE information is available in English. Formal licences, customs records and contracts should use terminology appropriate to the authority and commercial relationship.
Practical RiskAssuming that a Community Licence resolves Greek domestic authorisation, island-operation, port, ferry or customs requirements, or that an EORI identity eliminates contractual responsibility issues without separate operational assessment.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central practical risk is treating Greek logistics licensing and customs compliance as a one-time filing exercise rather than an ongoing operational and compliance process. Errors in Regional Unit jurisdiction, operator authorisation, vehicle-copy control, EORI documentation, island or port route design, or service documentation can affect timing, cost and the ability to operate as planned.

Regional Unit Competence RiskThe undertaking may submit the operator-authorisation or Community Licence application through an incorrect Regional Unit or fail to align its Greek registered office and operating model with the competent authority.
Licence Validity and Vehicle Copy RiskOperator authorisations and Community Licences have maximum five-year validity. A certified true copy is required for each vehicle engaged in international transport. Fleet additions, substitutions, expired evidence or document-control failures can create compliance exposure.
Professional Access RiskThe undertaking must maintain the applicable good-repute, financial-standing, professional-competence and establishment conditions. Shorter validity of a supporting document can affect the validity period of the authorisation.
Customs and EORI RiskIncorrect tariff classification, customs value, origin treatment, EORI data or procedure selection can affect duty, VAT, release timing and customs audit exposure. EORI application materials must be sent to the competent Customs Authority using the appropriate process.
Island and Port RiskIsland distribution, ferry capacity, port interfaces, maritime schedules, customs status and the allocation of goods control between shipper, carrier, forwarder, warehouse and 3PL provider can create operational and contractual disruption.
Cost DriversAuthorisation and licence fees, professional and financial evidence, certified vehicle copies, customs duties and import VAT, ferry, port and terminal charges, warehouse and 3PL fees, IT and declaration systems, island-route contingencies, professional advisers and possible penalties.

FAQ

Who grants authorisation to engage in road haulage in Greece?The Transport and Communications Department of the Regional Unit in which the transport undertaking has its registered office is competent to receive the application and supporting documents. The procedure is part of the Ministry of Infrastructure and Transport framework and can also be initiated at a KEP service point where applicable.
How long is a Greek road haulage operator authorisation valid?The authorisation has a maximum validity of five years. If a supporting document submitted for the application has a shorter validity period, the authorisation may be issued only for the period corresponding to that document's validity.
How does a Greek Community Licence work?The Community Licence enables eligible Greek transport undertakings to perform international road haulage under Regulation (EC) No 1072/2009. It is valid for five years, and a certified true copy is issued for each motor vehicle engaged in international transport. The copy must be available for controls.
When is an EORI number required in Greece?Economic operators involved in transactions governed by customs legislation need an EORI number. Once allocated by the competent Greek Customs Authority, the number can be used as the unique identification number in dealings with customs authorities across all EU member states.
Are freight forwarding and warehousing separately licensed in Greece?Freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics and 3PL services are generally organised through commercial contract and general Greek commercial law rather than a dedicated professional licence. Connected activities such as commercial road haulage, customs warehousing, customs representation, dangerous-goods handling or port operations may carry separate requirements.
Why do island logistics require separate planning?Greece's islands depend on ferry and maritime interfaces that can affect route availability, scheduling, capacity, terminal procedures, inventory planning, customs status and cost. The operational and contractual structure should account for the particular island, port, season, goods and mode of carriage.
