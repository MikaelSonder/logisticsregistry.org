Logistics and supply chain services in New Zealand cover the commercial function of planning, moving, storing, clearing and coordinating goods across road, rail, coastal and international sea, air and multimodal networks. The function includes goods carriage, freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics, third-party logistics (3PL), distribution, Customs handling and carrier coordination, together with the licensing, Customs, biosecurity and compliance layers that determine whether an operator may lawfully perform regulated activity.

New Zealand's island geography, distance from major markets, dependence on ports and airports, agricultural and food exports, strict biosecurity regime and dispersed domestic freight network make sea, air, road, warehouse, Customs and biosecurity planning central to the professional function. A Goods Service Licence is required for a service that delivers or carries goods — whether or not for hire or reward — using a motor vehicle with gross vehicle mass of 6,000 kg or more, including a vehicle hired to carry goods. The licence is administered by Waka Kotahi NZ Transport Agency. The operator or a person in control must be fit and proper, and the holder or controlling person must hold a certificate of knowledge of law and practice. Vehicles operating under the licence must display a Transport Service Licence label.

For international trade, the New Zealand Customs Service administers Customs declarations, client codes, supplier codes and Trade Single Window (TSW) participation. A client code is required for imports and exports valued at NZ$1,000 or more, food imports for sale or food ingredients irrespective of value, motor vehicle imports irrespective of value, and exports requiring a permit. A supplier code is also required for commercial import declarations. TSW is the electronic channel through which registered users submit information to and receive responses from Customs, the Ministry for Primary Industries, Maritime New Zealand and the Ministry of Health.

For international businesses, the New Zealand logistics environment should be assessed early alongside supply chain design, port, airport or warehouse selection, freight procurement, carrier and 3PL contracting, Customs and biosecurity strategy, TSW access and importer/exporter responsibility. A Goods Service Licence, client code, TSW registration or Customs clearance arrangement does not replace other approvals, and a change in vehicle, goods type, biosecurity status, facility, trade lane, declarant role or operating model can require a fresh regulatory and operational assessment.

Object Definition

Logistics and supply chain services in New Zealand form the commercial function concerned with the planning, physical movement, storage, Customs and biosecurity clearance and coordination of goods across domestic and international networks, and with determining which licences, registrations and compliance measures apply to a given service role, transport mode, vehicle, goods category, facility or trade lane. The function is broader than moving a shipment: it connects goods service licensing, freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics, 3PL coordination, Customs client and supplier codes, TSW lodgements, Customs classification, biosecurity clearance, transport documentation, liability rules and ongoing regulatory supervision.

DefinitionThe commercial and regulatory function covering logistics coordination, goods carriage, freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics, 3PL services, Customs and biosecurity clearance for goods moving into, out of or within New Zealand.

It focuses on the early classification question, the competent authority where regulation applies, the information base, the operational process and the practical consequences of a Goods Service Licence, TSW registration, client/supplier code, Customs procedure, biosecurity clearance, warehouse arrangement or freight-forwarding engagement.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of the logistics and supply chain function is to ensure that the movement, storage, forwarding, Customs and biosecurity clearance of goods are conducted reliably, lawfully and to commercial expectations, by entities holding the transport licences, border registrations, Customs status and operational capacity required for the service model. The function establishes how goods flow through the supply chain, which party is responsible for each stage, and what New Zealand transport, Customs, biosecurity, reporting and liability obligations apply.

PurposeTo ensure reliable, lawful and properly documented planning, movement, storage, forwarding, Customs and biosecurity clearance of goods within, into, out of or through New Zealand.
Primary OutcomeA functioning logistics or supply chain arrangement — supported, where relevant, by a Goods Service Licence, TSL vehicle labels, TSW user/declarant access, client and supplier codes, Customs procedure, biosecurity clearance or warehousing agreement — that defines the operational and legal position of the importer, exporter, carrier, forwarder, Customs broker, warehouse operator, 3PL provider, supplier or trader.
Business ValueWell-structured logistics arrangements and early regulatory clarity can reduce cargo delay, biosecurity holds, Customs errors, contractual liability exposure, working-capital disruption and later enforcement or audit risk.

Request Contexts, Users and Scenarios

Logistics and supply chain work is normally activated by a new New Zealand trade lane, domestic road freight model, warehouse requirement, port or airport interface, or entry into import, export, forwarding, Customs or biosecurity activity. The initial question is not simply whether a licence or code is required, but whether the undertaking acts as carrier, freight forwarder, declarant, customs broker, importer/exporter, supplier, warehouse operator, 3PL provider or trader, and whether its Goods Service Licence, client/supplier code and TSW position support the actual operating model.

Typical UserGoods service operators, freight forwarders, Customs brokers, warehouse, fulfilment and 3PL operators, contract-logistics providers, shipping agents, carriers, port and airport logistics participants, agricultural and food exporters/importers, manufacturers, e-commerce businesses, investors and foreign companies operating in New Zealand.
Business EventNew truck fleet or goods service, domestic distribution expansion, New Zealand import/export trade lane, port or airport warehouse establishment, 3PL contracting, Goods Service Licence application, client/supplier code request, TSW registration, food importer registration, new product import/export line, Customs warehouse arrangement or acquisition of a logistics business.
Typical ScenarioA carrier operates a 6,000 kg or heavier goods vehicle and applies to Waka Kotahi for a Goods Service Licence and TSL label; a commercial importer of goods over NZ$1,000 obtains client and supplier codes and registers with TSW before filing an import entry; an exporter uses TSW to lodge cargo and export information; a manufacturer appoints a 3PL provider for New Zealand warehousing and distribution and must allocate Customs, biosecurity, inventory and delivery responsibilities.
Professional AssistanceTypically relevant when carrier, importer/exporter, declarant or warehouse roles are uncertain, Goods Service Licence fit-and-proper requirements apply, tariff classification or Customs value is complex, biosecurity controls apply, or the supply chain spans several ports, airports, modes, providers and jurisdictions.

Country Characteristics

New Zealand's logistics environment is shaped by its island geography, export-oriented agricultural, forestry and food economy, dependence on sea and air routes, long internal supply lines, strict biosecurity framework and integrated electronic border systems. Waka Kotahi administers the Goods Service Licence, which is linked to vehicles of 6,000 kg gross vehicle mass or more and requires fit-and-proper persons plus knowledge of law and practice. The New Zealand Customs Service administers client and supplier codes and TSW, while MPI, Maritime New Zealand and the Ministry of Health participate in the same border information environment. A supply chain should therefore be designed around both movement and the full border and biosecurity sequence.

Operational CultureDocumentation-led, digitally administered, island-sensitive and biosecurity focused. Client codes, supplier codes, TSW entries, Customs, biosecurity, carrier, warehouse, port/airport and client data must remain consistent across border and operational systems.
Institutional StructureWaka Kotahi issues Goods Service Licences and TSL labels. New Zealand Customs Service administers Customs and client codes. TSW connects Customs, MPI, Maritime New Zealand and the Ministry of Health. Port, airport, local authority and product-control agencies regulate site and goods-specific matters.
Classification LogicA Goods Service Licence applies to a service carrying or delivering goods using a vehicle with gross vehicle mass of 6,000 kg or more, whether or not for hire or reward. Border requirements depend on value, goods type and the party's role: client code, supplier code, TSW access, Customs declaration, biosecurity permit or food-importer registration can all be relevant. Customs treatment depends on tariff classification, origin, value and selected procedure.
Language ExpectationEnglish is the primary working language for Waka Kotahi, Customs, TSW, MPI and commercial logistics documentation. Māori is an official language and can appear in government contexts, but English is ordinarily used for operational Customs, transport and trade records.

Applicable Legislation

New Zealand does not apply the EU Community Licence or Union Customs Code framework. Its logistics and supply chain function is governed by national transport, Customs, biosecurity, trade, vehicle, facility and product-control law. The central classification questions are the actual goods service, vehicle gross vehicle mass, importer/exporter and declarant roles, Customs/client/supplier code status, biosecurity and food status, warehouse model and trade lane. Freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics and 3PL arrangements are commercial functions, but they frequently connect to regulated transport, Customs, biosecurity and facility obligations.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

There is no single universal logistics process because the correct route depends on service role, transport mode, vehicle mass, goods category, biosecurity status, facility and trade lane. Nevertheless, most New Zealand operations move from supply-chain and role classification into entity and transport licensing, client-code and TSW setup, Customs/biosecurity preparation, facility and contract development, declarations and cargo release, and ongoing compliance.

1. Define the OperationIdentify whether the undertaking acts as goods carrier, freight forwarder, Customs broker, importer/exporter, supplier, warehouse operator, 3PL provider or trader; define vehicles, goods, facility, port/airport interface, Customs/biosecurity status and domestic/international trade lane.
2. Screen Regulatory TriggersAssess Goods Service Licence requirements at the 6,000 kg gross vehicle mass threshold, vehicle/driver rules, client and supplier codes, TSW registration, Customs declarations, tariff classification, biosecurity, food importer, Customs warehouse and other relevant requirements.
3. Identify the Competent Authority or CounterpartyDetermine whether the matter falls to Waka Kotahi, NZ Customs, TSW, MPI, Maritime NZ, a port or airport authority, Customs broker, freight forwarder, warehouse/3PL provider or product-control agency.
4. Establish Entity and Operating CapacityConfirm New Zealand entity or branch, authorised persons, goods service role, fit-and-proper person status, knowledge-of-law-and-practice certificate, vehicle/fleet arrangements, warehouse/3PL facilities and commercial contracts.
5. Obtain Transport and Border RegistrationsApply for a Goods Service Licence and obtain TSL labels where required; apply for client and supplier codes; register as a TSW User/declarant where direct filing is planned, or appoint a Customs broker, forwarder or agent.
6. Prepare Documentation and Goods ControlsDevelop invoices, packing lists, transport documents, tariff classification, Customs value and origin data, biosecurity conditions/permits, food import requirements, Customs declarations and freight/warehouse agreements.
7. Lodge Declarations and Cargo InformationSubmit import/export declarations, cargo reports and code applications through TSW or use an authorised broker/agent. Obtain applicable biosecurity, food, Customs or product-control approvals before release or export.
8. Clearance, Receipt and DeliveryComplete Customs and MPI clearance, duty/GST payment or procedure arrangements, cargo release, port/airport collection, warehouse receipt and delivery under the responsibilities defined in the operating model.
9. Operate, Monitor and Manage ChangeMaintain Goods Service Licence/TSL, TSW, client/supplier code, Customs, biosecurity, warehouse, transport and recordkeeping compliance; reassess before changing vehicles, goods, facilities, service roles or trade lanes.
Decision logic: First identify the legal and commercial role of every party, the vehicle mass and the goods' Customs/biosecurity status. Then determine whether the entity needs a Goods Service Licence, TSL label, client/supplier code, direct TSW registration or agent arrangement, and which Customs/biosecurity routes apply. Only after that classification can the documentation package, clearance sequence and contractual responsibilities be planned reliably.

Timeline

New Zealand logistics setup, Goods Service Licence preparation, TSW registration, Customs client-code and biosecurity planning should be treated as part of supply-chain design rather than late administrative tasks. Client-code processing begins only after New Zealand Customs receives the completed application and supporting documents. TSW User registration requires a RealMe login and, for declarants, approval of the relevant code. Import/export declaration timing is tied to cargo clearance, and biosecurity, food or product-controlled goods can require additional lead time.

Cross-Border Relevance

Logistics and supply chain operations in New Zealand are inherently cross-border because New Zealand depends on international sea and air trade and manages strict border biosecurity. New Zealand is outside the EU, EEA and EU Customs Union, so its Goods Service Licence, client/supplier code and TSW structure is distinct from EU Community Licence and EORI systems. The same commercial goods flow can require New Zealand Customs and MPI clearance, port or airport handling, domestic licensed road transport and separate overseas Customs, product and transport arrangements.

Foreign CompaniesA foreign group intending to directly undertake New Zealand import/export or logistics activity should determine whether a suitable New Zealand entity, client-code holder, importer/exporter, Customs broker, freight forwarder, carrier, warehouse or 3PL provider performs each regulated function. TSW, Customs, biosecurity, contract and goods-control roles should be aligned before operations start.
New Zealand-EU and International ContextNew Zealand does not use EU Community Licences or EORI numbers. New Zealand domestic goods service licensing is administered by Waka Kotahi; Customs and border identity use client/supplier codes and TSW. EU-side transport, EORI, VAT, Customs and product rules remain separate for goods moving into or through EU jurisdictions.
Island and Port/Airport ContextInternational goods flows depend on maritime and air routes, port/airport capacity and domestic road/rail distribution. Port, carrier, forwarder, warehouse, Customs and biosecurity roles must be allocated clearly, particularly where goods are held, treated or inspected before release.
Customs and Biosecurity ContextCustoms and biosecurity are connected but distinct border functions. A client/supplier code and TSW Customs declaration do not remove MPI requirements. Imports of food for sale or food ingredients require a client code regardless of value and may also require food importer registration and biosecurity compliance.
Language ConsiderationsEnglish is the principal working language for Customs, TSW, Waka Kotahi, MPI and commercial logistics documentation. International operations still require origin/destination country certificates, labels, declarations and Customs terminology in the form required by counterpart jurisdictions.
Practical RiskAssuming that an overseas importer-of-record, global 3PL contract, foreign Customs identifier or New Zealand client code alone resolves Goods Service Licence, TSW, Customs, biosecurity, facility or contractual responsibility issues without a separate operating-model assessment.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central practical risk is treating New Zealand logistics, Goods Service Licence rules, Customs declarations, TSW and biosecurity as separate administrative tasks rather than a single integrated operating model. Errors in identifying the importer, exporter, client-code holder, supplier, declarant, carrier, broker, warehouse or 3PL provider; inaccurate code, Customs or biosecurity data; or incomplete vehicle and facility control can affect cargo release, cost, working capital and the ability to operate as planned.

Goods Service Licence RiskA person may operate a goods service with a vehicle of 6,000 kg gross vehicle mass or more without a licence, fail the fit-and-proper person assessment, lack the required certificate of knowledge of law and practice or fail to display the required TSL label.
Client and Supplier Code RiskAn importer or exporter may not have a valid client code for a qualifying shipment, or may omit the supplier code needed for a commercial import declaration. This can interrupt the Customs entry and clearance process.
TSW Declarant and Role RiskThe commercial importer/exporter, client-code holder, TSW declarant, freight forwarder, Customs broker, carrier and warehouse operator may be different parties. Unclear authority, data ownership, Customs responsibility and liability allocation can create documentary and contractual risk.
Biosecurity RiskNew Zealand's biosecurity regime can apply to cargo, packaging, containers, machinery, food, plants, animals and related goods. A Customs declaration or cargo report does not remove MPI import conditions, permits, inspection, treatment or release requirements.
Customs Classification and Value RiskIncorrect tariff classification, Customs value, origin treatment, client/supplier code, procedure selection or free trade agreement claim can affect duty, GST, release timing, penalties and Customs audit exposure.
Warehouse, Port and Airport RiskUnclear allocation of goods control, Customs/biosecurity status, inventory, storage, port/airport handling and liability between importer, broker, forwarder, carrier, warehouse and 3PL provider can create operational and contractual disputes.
Cost DriversGoods Service Licence and vehicle costs, Customs duty and GST, biosecurity inspection/treatment, Customs broker and TSW systems, port/airport and shipping/air freight charges, warehouse and 3PL fees, domestic freight, professional advisers and potential penalties, storage or detention costs.

FAQ

When is a Goods Service Licence required in New Zealand?A Goods Service Licence is required for a service that delivers or carries goods, whether or not for hire or reward, using a motor vehicle with gross vehicle mass of 6,000 kg or more. The licence must be held before operating the service, and qualifying vehicles must display a TSL label.
What requirements apply to a Goods Service Licence?Waka Kotahi must be satisfied that any person who has or is likely to have control or involvement in the service is fit and proper. The licence holder or a person in control must also hold a certificate of knowledge of law and practice. The applicant specifies the type of goods service proposed.
When is a New Zealand Customs client code required?A client code is required for imports and exports valued at NZ$1,000 or more; imports of food for sale or food ingredients for use in food for sale regardless of value; motor vehicle imports regardless of value; and exports that require a permit regardless of value. Once issued, it must be used for eligible imports and exports.
What is a supplier code?A supplier code identifies the overseas supplier or relevant party in import declaration data. Commercial importers generally need both a client code and a supplier code when completing import declarations. Codes can be applied for and managed through TSW.
What is Trade Single Window (TSW)?TSW is the electronic channel through which approved users submit cargo and excise information and receive responses from border agencies, including New Zealand Customs Service, the Ministry for Primary Industries, Maritime New Zealand and the Ministry of Health. It also supports applications and management of importer, exporter and supplier codes.
Are freight forwarding and warehousing separately licensed in New Zealand?Freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics and 3PL services are generally structured through business registration, Customs/TSW roles, commercial contracts, facility requirements and the actual goods/transport model rather than a single universal logistics licence. Connected activities such as qualifying goods carriage, Customs brokerage, Customs-controlled storage, food importing, dangerous-goods handling or biosecurity-controlled storage may carry separate approvals and compliance requirements.

Operational Considerations

This section records the principal operational variables that commonly determine how a logistics or supply chain matter is classified, documented, examined and administered. The variables are registry-oriented reference points and do not determine the outcome of any individual case.

Operation DefinitionThe service role, transport mode, vehicle gross vehicle mass, goods type, Customs/biosecurity status, New Zealand entity, importer/exporter/client/supplier/declarant role, TSW model, port/airport/warehouse interface, trade lane and proposed changes should be described consistently across the record.
Goods Service RouteThe undertaking must determine whether the vehicle and service meet the 6,000 kg Goods Service Licence threshold, then establish fit-and-proper person status, certificate of knowledge of law and practice, TSL labels, vehicle records and the appropriate Waka Kotahi licensing route.
Customs and Biosecurity RouteThe entity must determine whether it will file through TSW directly or use a broker/agent; then establish client/supplier codes, importer/exporter role, tariff, value, origin, Customs procedure, duty/GST position, MPI import conditions, food/importer status, product approvals and release sequence.
Facility and Storage ModelOrdinary warehousing, Customs-controlled storage, biosecurity hold, fulfilment, contract logistics, 3PL, port/airport storage and bonded/approved warehouse arrangements have different Customs, tax, biosecurity, facility, inventory, security and contractual consequences.
Evidence BaseGoods Service Licence, TSL label, client/supplier code, TSW access, Customs declarations, invoices, packing lists, transport documents, tariff classification, value/origin evidence, biosecurity permits, warehouse agreements and inventory records form the documentary basis where relevant.
Decision ScopeA Goods Service Licence, TSL label, client/supplier code, TSW account, Customs/biosecurity clearance, warehouse approval or service agreement may define vehicle, goods, facility, reporting duties, Customs/biosecurity status, service levels and other conditions. The scope should be read with the underlying application, declaration or contractual material.
Change ManagementLater changes in fleet, vehicle mass, goods category, food/biosecurity status, trade lane, importer/exporter/declarant role, warehouse model, facility, ownership, client/supplier/TSW data or operating assumptions may require renewed assessment against existing licences, codes, declarations, Customs/biosecurity procedures or agreements.
