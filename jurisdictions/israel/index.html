Logistics and supply chain services in Israel cover the commercial function of planning, moving, storing, clearing and coordinating goods across road, rail, sea, air and multimodal networks. The function includes freight transport, freight forwarding, customs clearance, warehousing, contract logistics, third-party logistics (3PL), distribution, bonded storage and carrier coordination, together with the freight licence, customs, product-standard, security and compliance layers that determine whether an operator may lawfully perform the selected activity.

Israel's Mediterranean location, dependence on maritime and air trade, concentrated port and airport gateways, advanced technology and life-sciences sectors, and regional security environment make customs, port, airport, road freight, warehousing and goods-control planning central to the professional function. A carrier providing freight services must obtain a freight licence from the Ministry of Transport and Road Safety. The applicant must be an Israeli resident or a corporation registered in Israel, have commercial vehicle parking, employ a manager and meet management, training/certification and traffic safety officer requirements. The exact documentation differs by fleet size and hazardous-materials activity.

For international trade, the Customs and VAT Directorate of the Israel Tax Authority administers customs declarations, importer records, customs brokers, valuation, tariff classification, origin, import taxes, bonded arrangements and Authorised Economic Operator (AEO) procedures. Israel does not use the EU EORI system. Importers must maintain an importer declaration and use the prescribed Customs declaration route; customs brokers file import declarations based on documentation supplied by the importer. The Customs AEO programme covers supply-chain participants including exporters, warehouses, customs brokers, international forwarders, carriers, ports, air cargo terminals and airlines.

For international businesses, the Israeli logistics environment should be assessed early alongside supply chain design, port, airport or warehouse selection, freight procurement, carrier and 3PL contracting, customs strategy, importer-of-record arrangements, product standards and Hebrew-language administration. A freight licence, customs declaration, AEO status or warehouse arrangement does not replace other approvals. A change in fleet, hazardous-goods profile, goods category, product-standard status, facility, trade lane, customs procedure or operating model can require a fresh regulatory and operational assessment.

A properly organised and lawfully compliant logistics or supply chain operation in Israel — spanning freight transport, Customs clearance, forwarding, warehousing, bonded storage, port or air-cargo distribution, multimodal transport or 3PL activity — supported by the relevant freight licence, importer and customs records, product approvals, Customs procedure and commercial contracts.

Object Definition

Logistics and supply chain services in Israel form the commercial function concerned with the planning, physical movement, storage, Customs clearance and coordination of goods across domestic and international networks, and with determining which licences, Customs records, product approvals and compliance measures apply to a given service role, vehicle, goods category, facility or trade lane. The function is broader than moving a shipment: it connects licensed freight transport, freight forwarding, Customs brokerage, warehousing, bonded storage, contract logistics, 3PL coordination, Customs classification and valuation, importer declarations, transport documentation, liability rules and continuing regulatory supervision.

DefinitionThe commercial and regulatory function covering logistics coordination, freight transport, freight forwarding, warehousing, bonded storage, contract logistics, 3PL services and Customs clearance for goods moving into, out of or within Israel.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of the logistics and supply chain function is to ensure that the movement, storage, forwarding, Customs clearance and distribution of goods are conducted reliably, lawfully and to commercial expectations, by entities holding the freight licence, Customs status, product approvals, facility rights and operational capacity required for the selected service model. The function establishes how goods flow through the supply chain, which party is responsible for each stage, and what Israeli transport, Customs, standards, security, reporting and liability obligations apply.

PurposeTo ensure reliable, lawful and properly documented planning, movement, storage, forwarding, Customs clearance and distribution of goods within, into, out of or through Israel.
Primary OutcomeA functioning logistics or supply chain arrangement — supported, where relevant, by an Israeli freight licence, vehicle and safety records, importer declaration, Customs broker authority, Customs procedure, AEO or Customs warehouse status, product approval or warehouse agreement — that defines the operational and legal position of the importer, exporter, carrier, forwarder, broker, warehouse operator, 3PL provider or trader.
Business ValueWell-structured logistics arrangements and early regulatory clarity can reduce cargo delay, Customs and import-tax errors, standards holds, port/airport disruption, contractual liability exposure, working-capital interruption and later enforcement or audit risk.

Country Characteristics

Israel's logistics environment is shaped by its Mediterranean location, limited land-border trade channels compared with sea and air freight, concentration of cargo through major ports and airports, security-sensitive supply chains, advanced technology and life-sciences trade, and a detailed standards and import-control framework. The freight operator licence has a defined business and safety profile: an Israeli resident or locally registered corporation, commercial vehicle parking, an appropriately qualified manager and traffic safety officer are required. Customs, importer declaration, standards, Customs broker and facility roles must be aligned before goods move through the port, airport or warehouse chain.

Operational CultureDocumentation-led, security-sensitive, goods-specific and internationally connected. Carrier, importer, Customs broker, product-standard, port/airport, warehouse, safety officer and client data must remain consistent across Israeli authority and commercial systems.
Institutional StructureThe Ministry of Transport and Road Safety administers freight licences. Israel Tax Authority's Customs and VAT Directorate administers Customs, importer declarations, brokers and AEO. The Ministry of Economy and Standards Institution apply goods standards and import approval requirements. Port, airport, maritime and aviation authorities control facility and modal operations.
Classification LogicThe applicable route depends on whether the undertaking is a freight carrier, international forwarder, Customs broker, importer/exporter, warehouse/3PL operator or AEO participant; fleet size; hazardous goods; goods category; customs status; and facility. Freight licence management requirements differ for fleets of up to five versus six or more commercial freight vehicles. Customs treatment depends on classification, value, origin, procedure and product controls.
Language ExpectationHebrew is central to government filings, freight licensing, Customs and standards administration. English is extensively used in international trade, shipping, aviation and commercial logistics. Formal filings, declarations, certificates and contracts should use the language, data fields and terminology required by the competent Israeli authority.

Applicable Legislation

Israel does not apply the EU Community Licence or Union Customs Code framework. Its logistics and supply chain function is governed by Israeli road transport, Customs, import tax, product standards, security, port/airport, facility and goods-control law. The key classification questions are the actual business role, freight licence status, fleet and hazardous-goods profile, importer/exporter and Customs broker role, goods category, standards/conformity status, Customs procedure and facility model. Freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics and 3PL arrangements are commercial functions but can connect to regulated carrier, Customs, product-control and storage requirements.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

There is no single universal Israeli logistics process because the correct route depends on service role, vehicle fleet, goods category, product standards, Customs status, facility, port/airport and trade lane. Nevertheless, most operations move from supply-chain and role classification into local entity and freight licensing, Customs/importer setup, product and facility preparation, Customs declaration, release, storage or delivery, and ongoing compliance.

1. Define the OperationIdentify whether the undertaking acts as carrier, freight forwarder, international forwarder, Customs broker, importer/exporter, warehouse or bonded warehouse operator, 3PL provider or trader; define vehicles, goods, hazardous-goods profile, port/airport interface, facility, Customs and standards status and trade lane.
2. Screen Regulatory TriggersAssess freight licence, fleet size, parking, manager, traffic safety officer, hazardous materials, importer declaration, Customs broker, tariff/classification, Customs value/origin, product standards, Customs warehouse, AEO, port/airport and other relevant requirements.
3. Identify the Competent Authority or CounterpartyDetermine whether the matter falls to the Ministry of Transport Freight Division, Israel Tax Authority Customs and VAT Directorate, AEO programme, Standards Institution/Ministry of Economy, port/airport authority, Customs broker, warehouse/3PL provider or another product-control agency.
4. Establish Entity and Operating CapacityConfirm Israeli resident or locally registered corporate status where freight licensing is required; establish commercial vehicle parking, manager, traffic safety officer, vehicle fleet, warehouse/facility, authorised signatories and commercial contracts.
5. Prepare Customs, Standards and Documentation PositionIdentify the importer of record and Customs broker; prepare importer declaration, invoices, packing lists, transport documents, tariff classification, Customs value, origin, standards/conformity documents, AEO/bonded warehouse information and declaration data.
6. Apply, Register, Contract and CompleteApply for freight licence with the Ministry of Transport; appoint Customs broker or establish Customs filing position; obtain product approvals and facility status; conclude carrier, forwarding, warehouse and 3PL agreements.
7. Lodge Customs Declaration and Obtain ClearanceSubmit the Customs import/export declaration through the prescribed Customs/broker route; complete standards, product-control, duty/VAT, inspection, security and Customs release requirements.
8. Receive, Store, Distribute or ExportReceive cargo at port/airport, move it to the authorised warehouse or Customs-controlled facility, maintain inventory and document control, distribute domestically or export/re-export according to the selected Customs and contract model.
9. Operate, Monitor and Manage ChangeMaintain freight licence, safety, Customs, importer, product-standard, warehouse, AEO, vehicle, goods-control and recordkeeping compliance; reassess before changing fleet, hazardous materials, goods, facility, trade lane, customs procedure or service role.
Decision logic: First identify the Israeli entity, legal service role, goods and their Customs/standards/security status. Then determine whether the operation requires a freight licence, Customs broker, importer declaration, product approval, Customs warehouse, AEO or port/airport procedure. Only after that classification can the correct authority, document package, Customs route and contractual responsibilities be planned reliably.

Cross-Border Relevance

Logistics and supply chain operations in Israel are inherently cross-border because Israel depends substantially on sea and air trade and participates in global technology, pharmaceutical, industrial, agricultural and consumer-goods supply chains. Israel is outside the EU, EEA and EU Customs Union, so its freight licences, Customs declarations, importer records, AEO, standards and product-control processes are distinct from EU Community Licence and EORI systems. The same goods flow can require Israeli Customs and standards clearance, port/airport handling, domestic licensed freight transport and separate origin/destination Customs, product and transport arrangements.

Foreign CompaniesA foreign group intending to directly undertake Israeli import/export or logistics activity should determine whether a suitable Israeli entity, importer of record, licensed freight carrier, Customs broker, forwarder, warehouse or 3PL provider performs each regulated function. The local entity, freight licence, Customs, standards, contract and goods-control roles should be aligned before operations start.
Israel-EU and International ContextIsrael does not use EU Community Licences or EORI numbers. Israeli freight transport is licensed by the Ministry of Transport and Customs activity is administered by the Israel Tax Authority. EU-side transport, EORI, VAT, Customs and product rules remain separate for goods moving into or through EU jurisdictions.
Port and Airport ContextIsrael's international supply chains normally rely on sea and air gateways. Port/airport cargo, Customs, product approval, security, forwarder, Customs broker, carrier, warehouse and 3PL responsibilities should be explicitly allocated, particularly where cargo may be inspected, held or transferred under Customs control.
Customs, Standards and AEO ContextIsraeli Customs clearance can depend on Customs declaration data, importer declaration, tariff classification, Customs value, origin, standards and product approvals. AEO is a supply-chain programme encompassing exporters, warehouses, Customs brokers, international forwarders, carriers, ports, air cargo terminals and airlines; it does not replace product, Customs or operational compliance requirements.
Language ConsiderationsHebrew is central to government, Customs and standards procedures. English is widely used in international shipping, aviation, freight and commercial contracts. Cross-border documents must meet Israeli and counterpart-jurisdiction Customs, certificate-of-origin, labelling, product-control and language requirements.
Practical RiskAssuming that an overseas importer-of-record, global 3PL contract, foreign Customs identifier or international forwarder automatically resolves Israeli freight licensing, Customs broker, importer declaration, standards, port/airport, facility or contractual responsibility without a separate operating-model assessment.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central practical risk is treating Israeli logistics, freight licensing, Customs declarations, product standards, security and commercial contracts as separate administrative tasks rather than a single integrated operating model. Errors in identifying the importer, Customs broker, carrier, forwarder, warehouse, AEO participant or 3PL provider; inaccurate Customs/standards data; or incomplete vehicle and facility control can affect cargo release, cost, working capital and the ability to operate as planned.

Freight Licence and Fleet RiskA carrier may operate without a valid freight licence, local establishment, commercial vehicle parking, properly qualified manager, traffic safety officer or required vehicle documentation. Fleet growth can change the manager qualification route and should be reviewed before vehicles are deployed.
Hazardous Materials RiskTransport of hazardous materials requires separate personnel, permit and traffic safety officer documentation. A general freight licence does not remove hazardous-goods requirements.
Importer and Customs Broker Role RiskThe importer, Customs broker, carrier, forwarder, warehouse, Customs declaration party and goods owner may be different. Unclear authority, data ownership, Customs valuation, origin, standards responsibility and document control can create clearance and contractual risk.
Customs Classification, Value and Origin RiskIncorrect tariff classification, Customs value, supplier relationship data, origin treatment, importer declaration or procedure selection can affect import taxes, release timing, audit exposure and trade-agreement treatment.
Product Standards and Approval RiskGoods subject to mandatory standards, product conformity, food, medical, pharmaceutical, plant/animal, telecommunications, defence or other regulatory control can be held until the appropriate approval, declaration, certificate or Customs code is provided.
Port, Airport and Warehouse RiskUnclear allocation of goods control, Customs/standards status, inventory, security, terminal interfaces and liability between importer, broker, forwarder, carrier, warehouse and 3PL provider can create operational delay and contractual disputes.
Cost DriversFreight licence, manager, safety and vehicle costs; Customs duties and import taxes; Customs broker and declaration costs; standards testing/certification; port/airport and terminal charges; warehouse and 3PL fees; security, IT and inventory systems; professional advisers; and potential penalties, storage or demurrage.

FAQ

Who needs a freight licence in Israel?A person or company intending to provide freight services must obtain a freight licence from the Ministry of Transport and Road Safety. The applicant must be an Israeli resident or a corporation registered in Israel, have commercial vehicle parking, employ a manager and meet the applicable traffic safety and professional requirements.
How does fleet size affect the Israeli freight licence?For a fleet of up to five motorised freight vehicles, the freight manager must pass the Ministry of Transport freight training course. For six or more motorised freight vehicles, the freight manager must hold a certification in freighting. The applicant must also be under the supervision of a traffic safety officer.
What must be filed for an Israeli commercial import?The importer or its Customs broker lodges the required Customs import declaration with the supporting commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or air waybill, tariff classification, Customs value, origin, importer declaration and any applicable standards or product-control documents. The exact document set depends on the goods and Customs procedure.
Does Israel use EORI?No. EORI is an EU Customs identification system. Israel uses its own Customs and VAT Directorate procedures, importer records, Customs broker framework and Customs declaration data. An EU EORI may still be relevant for the EU-side part of a supply chain, but it is not an Israeli Customs identifier.
What is Israel's Customs AEO programme?The Authorized Economic Operator programme is a Customs security and compliance framework for supply-chain participants. It covers parties such as exporters, warehouses, Customs brokers, international forwarders, carriers, ports, air cargo terminals and airlines. It is separate from, and does not replace, freight licensing, Customs declarations, product approvals or facility requirements.
Are freight forwarding and warehousing separately regulated in Israel?Freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics and 3PL services are generally structured through business, Customs, facility and commercial contract arrangements rather than one universal logistics licence. Connected activities such as licensed road freight, Customs brokerage, Customs-controlled storage, hazardous-goods handling, product-standard compliance, port/airport operations or AEO participation may carry separate approvals and compliance requirements.
