Logistics and supply chain services in Brazil cover the commercial function of planning, moving, storing, clearing and coordinating goods across road, rail, inland waterway, coastal and international sea, air and multimodal networks. The function includes paid road freight, freight forwarding, customs brokerage, warehousing, contract logistics, third-party logistics (3PL), distribution, import/export operations and carrier coordination, together with the registration, tax, customs, product-control and compliance layers that determine whether an operator may lawfully perform the selected activity.

Brazil's continental scale, federal-state structure, reliance on road freight, major agricultural, mining, manufacturing and consumer markets, Atlantic ports, air cargo, inland waterways and Mercosur trade links make domestic distribution and international logistics operationally complex. Paid road freight transport is regulated by the National Land Transport Agency (ANTT). Registration in the National Register of Road Freight Carriers (RNTRC) is mandatory for carriers transporting cargo for remuneration, whether registered as an autonomous cargo carrier (TAC), road freight transport company (ETC) or freight transport cooperative (CTC). RNTRC Digital is the principal online route for registration, fleet management, reactivation and updates.

For foreign trade, the Federal Revenue of Brazil (Receita Federal) administers customs and tax functions, while the Secretariat of Foreign Trade (SECEX) administers foreign-trade policy functions. Brazilian importers and exporters use the Integrated Foreign Trade System (Siscomex) and the Single Portal (Portal Único). A Brazilian company needs a CNPJ and customs qualification, commonly known as RADAR habilitação, to operate directly in Siscomex. RADAR is linked to the company's CNPJ and is assessed according to the operator's capacity. Import and export declarations are processed through Siscomex modules, including the newer DUIMP import declaration environment and export declaration routes.

For international businesses, the Brazilian logistics environment should be assessed early alongside Brazilian entity and CNPJ structure, RNTRC category, warehouse and port/airport selection, freight procurement, carrier and 3PL contracting, RADAR/Siscomex readiness, customs broker role, import/export responsibility, tax and Mercosur strategy. An RNTRC registration, CNPJ, RADAR authorisation or Siscomex declaration does not replace other approvals. A change in fleet, carrier category, goods type, tax status, facility, trade lane, customs procedure or operating model can require a fresh regulatory and operational assessment.

A properly organised and lawfully compliant logistics or supply chain operation in Brazil — spanning paid road freight, customs clearance, freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics, port, inland-waterway or air-cargo distribution, multimodal transport or 3PL activity — supported by the relevant RNTRC registration, CNPJ/RADAR/Siscomex status, customs procedure, tax position and commercial contracts.

Object Definition

Logistics and supply chain services in Brazil form the commercial function concerned with the planning, physical movement, storage, customs clearance and coordination of goods across domestic and international networks, and with determining which registrations, tax identities, permits, customs qualifications and compliance measures apply to a given service role, vehicle, goods category, facility or trade lane. The function is broader than moving a shipment: it connects RNTRC road freight registration, freight forwarding, customs brokerage, warehousing, contract logistics, 3PL coordination, CNPJ, RADAR, Siscomex, customs classification, declarations, transport documentation, liability rules and continuing regulatory supervision.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of the logistics and supply chain function is to ensure that the movement, storage, forwarding and customs clearance of goods are conducted reliably, lawfully and to commercial expectations, by entities holding the road freight registrations, Brazilian tax/trade identities, customs qualifications and operational capacity required for the service model. The function establishes how goods flow through the supply chain, which party is responsible for each stage, and what Brazilian transport, customs, tax, reporting and liability obligations apply.

PurposeTo ensure reliable, lawful and properly documented planning, movement, storage, forwarding and customs clearance of goods within, into, out of or through Brazil.
Primary OutcomeA functioning logistics or supply chain arrangement — supported, where relevant, by RNTRC registration, CNPJ/RADAR/Siscomex qualification, customs broker authority, import/export declaration, tax documentation, warehouse arrangement or service agreement — that defines the operational and legal position of the carrier, forwarder, customs broker, importer, exporter, warehouse operator, 3PL provider or trader.
Business ValueWell-structured logistics arrangements and early regulatory clarity can reduce cargo delay, customs and tax errors, contractual liability exposure, working-capital interruption and later enforcement or audit risk.

Country Characteristics

Brazil's logistics environment is shaped by continental scale, varied infrastructure, a high reliance on road transport, federal-state tax and regulatory layers, major commodity and industrial supply chains, Atlantic ports, Mercosur integration and complex foreign trade procedures. The professional road freight route centres on RNTRC, a mandatory national registration for paid freight carriers in the TAC, ETC and CTC categories. The foreign-trade route centres on a Brazilian CNPJ, RADAR qualification and Siscomex/Portal Único use. Those transport, tax and Customs systems must be treated as connected, not interchangeable, components of the same logistics operating model.

Operational CultureDocumentation-led, tax/customs integrated, digitally administered and geographically varied. CNPJ, RNTRC, vehicle, freight document, tax, RADAR, Siscomex, Customs, warehouse, port/airport and client data must remain consistent across federal, state and commercial systems.
Institutional StructureANTT regulates and supervises paid national and international road freight and RNTRC. Receita Federal administers customs and RADAR/Siscomex qualification. SECEX governs aspects of foreign trade policy. State authorities remain relevant to ICMS, vehicle, facility, environmental and operational matters. Port, airport, waterway and product agencies apply modal and goods-specific controls.
Classification LogicThe first classification distinguishes paid road freight from own-account movement and identifies the RNTRC category: TAC, ETC or CTC. The foreign-trade classification then identifies the Brazilian entity, CNPJ, RADAR/Siscomex qualification, importer/exporter, customs broker, tariff, value, origin, special regime, goods control and Customs procedure. The facility classification distinguishes ordinary warehouse, Customs-controlled or special regime arrangements.
Language ExpectationPortuguese is central to ANTT, Receita Federal, Siscomex, tax, customs, legal and authority procedures. English is used in international shipping and commercial logistics, but formal registrations, declarations, customs records, tax documents and contracts should use precise Portuguese terminology or professionally controlled translations.

Applicable Legislation

Brazil does not apply the EU Community Licence or Union Customs Code framework. Its logistics and supply chain function is governed by Brazilian road transport, customs, foreign trade, tax, port, facility and product-control law, with material federal and state interfaces. The central classification questions are the actual paid carrier category, CNPJ/tax status, importer/exporter role, RADAR/Siscomex qualification, customs broker authority, goods category, Customs procedure, facility and trade lane. Freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics and 3PL arrangements are commercial functions but connect to regulated transport, Customs, tax and product-control routes.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

There is no single universal Brazilian logistics process because the correct route depends on the service role, carrier category, state, transport mode, goods, tax status, Customs procedure, facility and trade lane. Nevertheless, most operations move from supply-chain and role classification into Brazilian entity/CNPJ setup, RNTRC or transport preparation, RADAR/Siscomex qualification, facility and contract development, Customs declaration, cargo release or delivery and continuing compliance.

1. Define the OperationIdentify whether the undertaking acts as autonomous cargo carrier, transport company, cooperative, freight forwarder, Customs broker, importer/exporter, warehouse operator, 3PL provider or trader; define vehicles, states, goods, facility, port/airport/waterway interface and domestic/international trade lane.
2. Screen Transport, Tax and Trade TriggersAssess paid freight/RNTRC category, own-account versus third-party transport, ANTT requirements, CNPJ, tax/GST-like state taxes, RADAR/Siscomex, Customs broker, tariff, value, origin, import/export, special regime, product controls, port/airport and other relevant routes.
3. Identify the Competent Authority or CounterpartyDetermine whether the matter falls to ANTT, Receita Federal, SECEX, Siscomex/Portal Único, state tax authority, port/airport Customs, Customs broker, warehouse/3PL provider, ANTAQ or product-control agency.
4. Establish Brazilian Entity, CNPJ and Operating CapacityConfirm Brazilian company, branch or authorised importer structure; obtain/verify CNPJ, tax registrations, digital certificate, authorised personnel, carrier category, fleet, facility, warehouse/3PL arrangement and commercial contracts.
5. Obtain RNTRC and Foreign Trade QualificationsRegister as TAC, ETC or CTC in RNTRC Digital before undertaking paid road freight; manage fleet data; obtain RADAR habilitação linked to the CNPJ for direct Siscomex access; identify Customs broker and importer/exporter roles.
6. Prepare Documentation and Goods ControlsDevelop RNTRC records, vehicle and freight documents, commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading/air waybill/road document, tariff classification, Customs value, origin, import/export licence, DUIMP or other declaration data, tax records and freight/warehouse agreements.
7. Lodge Siscomex and Customs DeclarationsFile the relevant import or export declaration through Siscomex/Portal Único directly or through an authorised Customs broker; submit cargo, licensing, tax and product-control information; complete port, airport, border or Customs warehouse formalities.
8. Clearance, Delivery and ReconciliationComplete Customs assessment, duties and taxes, inspection, cargo release, warehouse receipt, domestic or international transport and delivery; reconcile RNTRC, CNPJ, RADAR, Siscomex, Customs, tax, invoice, freight and commercial records.
9. Operate, Monitor and Manage ChangeMaintain RNTRC, CNPJ, tax, RADAR, Siscomex, Customs, vehicle, freight, warehouse, product-control and recordkeeping compliance; reassess before changing fleet, carrier category, goods, facilities, states, importer/exporter roles or trade lanes.
Decision logic: First identify the Brazilian entity, actual service role, whether freight is paid third-party transport, and the goods' customs/tax/product status. Then determine RNTRC category, CNPJ/RADAR/Siscomex qualification, importer/exporter and Customs broker role, Customs procedure, facility and route. Only after that classification can the correct authority, documentation package, declaration sequence and contractual responsibilities be planned reliably.

Timeline

Brazilian logistics setup, RNTRC registration, CNPJ/RADAR/Siscomex qualification, Customs and facility planning should be treated as part of supply-chain design rather than late administrative tasks. RNTRC Digital enables the carrier to register and manage its fleet online, but the relevant carrier-category documentation must be complete. Direct foreign trade requires a Brazilian CNPJ and RADAR habilitação before Siscomex operations begin. Customs clearance timing depends on the Customs regime, goods, port/airport, classification, valuation, licensing, product controls, Customs channel and document completeness.

Cross-Border Relevance

Logistics and supply chain operations in Brazil are inherently cross-border because Brazil is a major global commodity, industrial, consumer and services market with Atlantic ports, land borders and Mercosur trade links. Brazil is outside the EU, EEA and EU Customs Union, so RNTRC, CNPJ, RADAR, Siscomex and Brazilian Customs procedures are distinct from EU Community Licence and EORI systems. The same goods flow can require Brazilian carrier registration, CNPJ and RADAR qualification, Siscomex declaration, Customs broker authority, Customs/tax/product clearance, Mercosur origin evidence and separate counterpart-jurisdiction requirements.

Foreign CompaniesA foreign group intending to directly undertake Brazilian import/export, logistics or distribution activity should determine whether a suitable Brazilian entity, CNPJ holder, RNTRC carrier, RADAR-qualified importer/exporter, Customs broker, forwarder, warehouse or 3PL provider performs each regulated function. A foreign company cannot ordinarily obtain RADAR without a Brazilian company with CNPJ and the corresponding domestic tax/customs standing.
Brazil-EU and International ContextBrazil does not use EU Community Licences or EORI numbers. Brazilian paid road freight is governed by RNTRC; direct import/export customs access is based on CNPJ and RADAR/Siscomex. EU-side road transport, EORI, VAT, Customs and product rules remain separate for goods moving into or through EU jurisdictions.
Mercosur ContextMercosur and other preferential trade arrangements can affect origin, tariff and customs treatment. A preferential claim must be supported by appropriate origin and Customs documentation and does not replace Brazilian RNTRC, CNPJ, RADAR, Siscomex, Customs broker, tax or product-control requirements.
Port, Land Border and Inland Waterway ContextBrazil's Atlantic ports, land borders, rail corridors and inland waterways produce different cargo, Customs, terminal, transit, tax and transport arrangements. The specific port, airport, border or inland customs/facility route should be assessed for the goods and service model.
Customs and Siscomex ContextDirect Brazilian foreign trade depends on CNPJ-linked RADAR habilitação and Siscomex access. The operator, importer/exporter, Customs broker, declarant, warehouse and goods owner roles must be aligned with the declaration and Customs procedure before the goods enter clearance.
Language ConsiderationsPortuguese is central to Brazilian transport, tax, Customs, Siscomex, legal and authority interaction. English is widely used in international shipping and commodities trade, but formal RNTRC records, declarations, Customs documents, tax documents and contracts should use precise Portuguese terminology and meet Brazilian requirements.
Practical RiskAssuming that a foreign importer-of-record, global 3PL contract, overseas Customs identifier, Mercosur claim or overseas carrier authority automatically creates Brazilian RNTRC, CNPJ, RADAR, Siscomex, Customs broker, Customs tax or contractual authority without a separate Brazilian operating-model assessment.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central practical risk is treating Brazilian logistics, RNTRC, CNPJ/RADAR/Siscomex, Customs, tax and commercial contracts as separate administrative tasks rather than a single integrated operating model. Errors in carrier category, entity identity, tax/customs qualification, declaration, value/origin, product compliance, freight payment, warehouse arrangement or contract allocation can affect cargo release, cost, working capital and the ability to operate as planned.

RNTRC Carrier Category RiskA paid freight carrier may operate without RNTRC registration or under an incorrect TAC, ETC or CTC category. RNTRC applies specifically to paid road freight; own-account transport and commercial transport should not be conflated without checking the statutory activity.
Fleet and Freight Documentation RiskAll cargo motor vehicles and road implements used in paid freight operations must be appropriately registered and maintained in RNTRC. Fleet additions, removals, freight contracts, mandatory electronic freight payment, toll vouchers and transport documentation require ongoing control.
CNPJ, RADAR and Siscomex RiskA company may be unable to operate directly in foreign trade if CNPJ, tax status, digital certificate, RADAR qualification, authorised users or Siscomex data are absent, inactive or inconsistent. RADAR is linked to the Brazilian CNPJ and its qualification can be constrained by the operator's capacity profile.
Customs, Tax, Value and Origin RiskIncorrect tariff classification, Customs value, origin treatment, importer data, Customs procedure, tax calculation, declaration information or special-regime use can affect duty, federal taxes, ICMS, release timing, penalties and Customs audit exposure.
Product, Sanitary and Environmental RiskA Customs declaration or RADAR qualification does not remove product-specific controls. Agricultural, food, animal/plant, pharmaceutical, health, chemical, dangerous goods, environmental, technical and export-controlled products can require separate licences, registrations, inspection or authority clearance.
State, Port and Warehouse RiskState taxes, road conditions, port/airport capacity, Customs channel, storage, demurrage, Customs warehouse, special regimes, inventory and freight document requirements can create operational and contractual disruption if not integrated into route and warehouse planning.
Cost DriversRNTRC and fleet compliance, CNPJ/RADAR/Siscomex and digital systems, Customs duties and federal/state taxes, Customs broker fees, port/airport/terminal charges, warehouse and 3PL fees, tolls, mandatory freight/toll arrangements, product approvals, professional advisers and potential penalties, storage or demurrage.

FAQ

What is RNTRC?RNTRC is the National Register of Road Freight Carriers administered by ANTT. Registration is mandatory for paid road freight carriers in one of three categories: autonomous cargo carrier (TAC), road freight transport company (ETC) or freight transport cooperative (CTC). It applies to transport carried out for remuneration.
Can RNTRC registration be managed online?Yes. RNTRC Digital allows the carrier to request registration, manage its fleet by adding or removing vehicles, reactivate registration and update information directly online without needing to attend an ANTT accredited service point in many cases.
What is RADAR habilitação?RADAR is the Receita Federal qualification that enables a Brazilian company or individual to operate in Siscomex for import/export activity. It is linked to the Brazilian CNPJ or tax identity and is a prerequisite for direct foreign trade Customs operations. The appropriate authorisation and capacity position should be confirmed before shipments are planned.
What is Siscomex?Siscomex is Brazil's Integrated Foreign Trade System. It is the electronic environment through which import/export operations, customs declarations, cargo information, licences and connected foreign trade procedures are registered and processed. The Single Portal is the integrated digital environment supporting newer foreign trade processes.
Can a foreign company obtain RADAR directly?A foreign company normally needs an appropriate Brazilian legal presence and CNPJ to obtain RADAR habilitação for direct Brazilian import/export operations. Foreign groups frequently use a Brazilian subsidiary, qualified importer of record, Customs broker or other lawful local operating model. The correct structure depends on the actual transaction, tax, Customs and contractual roles.
Are freight forwarding and warehousing separately licensed in Brazil?Freight forwarding, warehousing, contract logistics and 3PL are commercial functions, but the actual model can connect to separate registrations and approvals. Paid road freight requires RNTRC; Customs brokers have their own customs authorisation; Customs warehouses, ports, bonded facilities, dangerous goods, controlled products, state tax registrations and specific facilities can require separate licences, permits or compliance conditions.

Operational Considerations

This section records the principal operational variables that commonly determine how a logistics or supply chain matter is classified, documented, examined and administered. The variables are registry-oriented reference points and do not determine the outcome of any individual case.

Operation DefinitionThe service role, carrier category, paid versus own-account freight model, transport mode, vehicles, Brazilian states, goods type, Brazilian entity, CNPJ/RNTRC/RADAR/Siscomex status, importer/exporter/broker role, port/airport/warehouse interface, trade lane and proposed changes should be described consistently across the record.
Road Freight Registration RouteThe undertaking must determine whether it performs paid third-party road freight and, if so, establish the appropriate RNTRC category (TAC, ETC or CTC), register its fleet and maintain the carrier, vehicle, freight contract, toll and payment documentation required for the activity.
Customs and Foreign Trade IdentityBrazilian entity, CNPJ, tax status, digital certificate, RADAR qualification, Siscomex access, importer/exporter role, Customs broker authority, tariff, value, origin, Customs procedure, declaration and product-control position should be aligned before goods enter foreign trade clearance.
Facility and Storage ModelOrdinary warehousing, Customs warehouse, fulfilment, contract logistics, 3PL, port/airport storage, special Customs regime, bonded or Customs-controlled arrangements have different Customs, tax, facility, inventory, security and contractual consequences.
Evidence BaseRNTRC registration, carrier category, vehicle/fleet records, CNPJ, tax status, RADAR/Siscomex access, import/export declarations, invoices, packing lists, transport documents, tariff classification, value/origin evidence, product approvals, freight agreements, warehouse agreements and inventory records form the documentary basis where relevant.
Decision ScopeAn RNTRC registration, RADAR qualification, Siscomex access, import/export declaration, Customs procedure, special regime, facility approval or service agreement may define operating role, vehicle, goods, facility, Customs/tax position, reporting duties, service levels and other conditions. The scope should be read with the underlying registration, declaration or contractual material.
